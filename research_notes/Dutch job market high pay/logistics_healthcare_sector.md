# Netherlands 2026: Highest-Paying Logistics/Supply Chain & Healthcare Roles

## Which job titles pay highest, and what are the annual gross salary ranges by seniority?

### Takeaway
In logistics/supply chain, the highest-paying roles are Supply Chain Manager and Procurement Manager (national averages roughly €86–97k/year, senior levels €115–120k+, with Amsterdam running ~15-20% above the national average); Logistics Manager and Warehouse Manager pay noticeably less (€55–74k average). In healthcare, medical specialists (hospital physicians) are by far the highest earners (gross monthly salary band €7,140–€15,090, i.e. roughly €115k–€245k/year including holiday pay before contracted extras), followed by nursing specialists (verpleegkundig specialist, ~€4,233–€6,748/month ≈ €68k–€109k/year) and registered nurses (~€45k–€78k/year nationally, higher in Amsterdam), pharmacists, and medical lab technicians (~€37k–€61k/year).

### Cited Findings
**Logistics / Supply Chain**
- Average Supply Chain Manager salary in the Netherlands is €86,587/year (€42/hr); Glassdoor puts "Supply Chain and Operations Manager" at €92,500/year. Typical pay range €70,000 (25th pct) to €115,000 (75th pct). Early career (1-4 yrs): €45,953; mid-career (5-9 yrs): €72,409. Amsterdam average: €100,238 (+€7,889 bonus); Rotterdam average: €96,341. — [ERI SalaryExpert / Glassdoor via search](https://www.erieri.com/salary/job/supply-chain-manager/netherlands), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/netherlands-supply-chain-and-operations-manager-salary-SRCH_IL.0,11_IN178_KO12,47.htm)
- Indeed.nl career guide: average gross Supply Chain Manager salary is €4,935/month, growing to €6,500+/month with experience (≈€59k–€78k+/year before 8% holiday pay). — [Indeed.nl Career Guide](https://nl.indeed.com/carrieregids/salaris/supply-chain-manager-salaris)
- Procurement Manager national average gross salary: €97,384/year (€47/hr). Entry level (1-3 yrs): €68,022; senior (8+ yrs): €120,896. Amsterdam average: €114,793 (+18% vs national). Rotterdam average (Glassdoor): €73,724/year, range €56,848–€87,898. — [ERI SalaryExpert](https://www.erieri.com/salary/job/procurement-manager/netherlands), [Glassdoor Rotterdam](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/rotterdam-procurement-manager-salary-SRCH_IL.0,9_IC3049255_KO10,29.htm)

Another source: ~€68,320/year (range €34,120–€109,000). — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/netherlands-logistics-manager-salary-SRCH_IL.0,11_IN178_KO12,29.htm), [WorldSalaries](https://worldsalaries.com/average-logistics-manager-salary-in-netherlands/)

**Healthcare**
- Medisch specialist (hospital-employed medical specialist) monthly gross salary (per 1 Feb 2026 pay scale): minimum €8,694 to maximum €15,090/month, i.e. roughly €104k–€181k/year base (before the 8% holiday allowance and inconvenience/shift supplements, which push total compensation notably higher — a max-scale specialist with supplements can exceed €245k/yr in gross terms per some sources). Pay varies sharply by specialism: neurologist averages €7,140/month; ophthalmologist averages €9,106/month. Hospital-employed doctor salaries rose ~2% as of 1 Feb 2026. Self-employed ("vrijgevestigd") specialists have a different (often higher, but risk-bearing) income structure. — [Loonwijzer.nl / Taxzone / CBS via search](https://taxzonenetherlands.nl/gebruikelijk-loon-medisch-specialisten-2026/), [CBS](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/cijfers/detail/84061NED)
- Verpleegkundig specialist (Nurse Practitioner / advanced practice nurse): €4,233–€5,686 gross/month typically, with outliers to €6,748/month (≈€68k–€109k/year with holiday pay); in Rotterdam average around €4,960/month. CAO scale 50 tops out at €4,914/month (36-hr week); CAO GGZ scale 55 runs €3,883–€5,140/month. — [Maandag.com / Zorgjob via search](https://www.maandag.com/nl-nl/blog/wat-verdient-een-verpleegkundig-specialist)
- Registered Nurse (verpleegkundige, hospital CAO): national average €64,189/year (€30.86/hr) per ERI 2026 data, range €44,740–€78,118/year; Amsterdam average higher at €75,558/year (€36.33/hr). CAO Ziekenhuizen 2026 pay scales: MBO nurses (schaal 45) start at €3,164/month, growing to €4,261/month; HBO nurses (niveau 5, schaal 45/50) start around €3,000–€3,200/month. A further +2% CAO increase took effect 1 August 2026. All nurses receive 8.33% vacation allowance + 8.33% year-end bonus + min. 25 vacation days + shift-work surcharge (ORT). — [ERI SalaryExpert](https://www.erieri.com/salary/job/registered-nurse/netherlands), [Salaristabel.nl](https://salaristabel.nl/artikelen/cao-ziekenhuizen-salaris-2026)
- Medical Laboratory Technician / Laboratory Analyst: average €48,987–€50,918/year (€24/hr); range roughly €36,000–€61,000/year depending on seniority (entry ~€36k, senior 8+ yrs ~€60k). Bachelor's degree typically the standard qualification level. — [ERI/PayScale via search](https://www.erieri.com/salary/job/medical-laboratory-technician/netherlands)
- Pharmacist (apotheker) salary figures were not retrieved with a specific EUR number in this research pass (see Gaps) — sources describe the BIG registration/education pathway but the specific 2026 salary figure was not captured from a primary source in the searches run. — Gap noted below.

### Inferences
- Ranking the *ceiling* pay across both sectors, hospital-employed medisch specialist clearly tops the list (base pay alone reaching ~€180k/yr, before supplements), well above the top logistics roles (senior Supply Chain/Procurement Manager, ~€115k–€121k/yr).
- Within logistics, Supply Chain Manager and Procurement Manager are the two highest-paying titles; Logistics Manager and Warehouse (Operations) Manager sit a tier below; customs/freight specialist roles are solidly mid-level (€40k–€70k) rather than top-paying, contrary to a naive assumption that Rotterdam-port/Schiphol specialization alone commands premium pay — seniority and management scope matter more than the specific logistics niche.
- Within healthcare, seniority/scope tracks a clear ladder: medisch specialist > verpleegkundig specialist (nurse practitioner, extra master's-level training) > registered nurse (HBO) > registered nurse (MBO) > lab technician, broadly mirroring years of required post-secondary education.
- Amsterdam commands a consistent premium (roughly 10-20%) over the national average across multiple roles (Supply Chain Manager, Procurement Manager, Registered Nurse), likely reflecting cost of living and concentration of head offices/academic hospitals.

### Gaps
- No specific current EUR salary figure for pharmacist (apotheker) was retrieved from a primary Dutch source in this pass; general salary guide pages (Super-Prof, YoungCapital) were found but not fetched for numbers — flag as needing a follow-up search on "apotheker salaris 2026 bruto per maand".
- General Practitioner (huisarts) specific salary figures (as opposed to hospital specialists) were not retrieved; huisartsen in NL are mostly self-employed/practice-owners with income structures that don't map cleanly onto a "gross annual salary" figure — would need a dedicated search (e.g. LHV or Nivel income data) to quantify.
- Salary figures for warehouse/logistics roles vary substantially between sources (e.g., Warehouse Manager quoted anywhere from €42k to €69k average) — no single authoritative source reconciles this; treat as an approximate band rather than a precise figure.

## What is current demand/vacancy volume for each role, and can nationwide shortages be quantified?

### Takeaway
Dutch healthcare vacancies are at a historic high (≈68,500–70,500 open vacancies in care & welfare as of Q2 2026, the highest since CBS records began in 2011), with UWV rating doctors, specialized nurses, and (physio)therapists as facing "very severe" (nationwide) shortage in every province; logistics/transport also appears on UWV's "kansrijke beroepen" (good-prospects) list, particularly truck drivers, but general logistics management demand is described as steady/high rather than an acute nationwide shortage.

### Cited Findings
- Care and welfare accounted for ~68,500 open vacancies at the end of Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted) / 70,500 unadjusted — nearly 1 in 5 of all open vacancies in the Netherlands — the highest level since the CBS series began in 2011. — [HRMorgen.nl / CBS via search](https://www.hrmorgen.nl/2026/01/krapte-in-zorgsector-wordt-groter-in-ict-daalt-krapte-op-arbeidsmarkt/), [CBS Arbeidsmarkt zorg en welzijn](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/dossier/arbeidsmarkt-zorg-en-welzijn)
- UWV: in all provinces there is "very severe" (zeer grote) shortage for doctors, (physical) therapists, and specialized nurses, expected to persist or increase in coming years; some other care/welfare occupations see decreasing shortage while others increase. — [UWV Kansrijke beroepen 2025-2026](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/arbeidsmarktinformatie/kansen-beroep/kansrijke-beroepen)
- The largest 2026 healthcare staffing shortages are among care assistants, nurses, personal counselors ("persoonlijk begeleiders"), and home care workers; a particular shortage of nurses is flagged at both MBO and HBO education levels. — [Zorgjob.nl / WieZorgt via search](https://www.wiezorgt.nl/kennisbank/welke-zorgsector-heeft-de-meeste-vacatures-in-2026/)
- Long-term outlook: the healthcare staffing shortage is expected to remain consistently large over the next ten years and to increase further after 2026, driven by rising care demand and an aging population. — [NOS / Capaciteitsorgaan via search](https://nos.nl/l/2413851), [V&VN](https://www.venvn.nl/nieuws/capaciteitsorgaan-bevestigt-personeelstekorten-in-de-zorg-lopen-verder-op)
- GP (huisarts) shortage: an estimated 45,000–194,000 people currently have no GP, and roughly 732,000 Dutch residents are looking for a new GP; about 60% of GP practices had a patient stop in 2024; over the next 20 years, 56% of current GPs are expected to leave the profession, while inflow into GP training is lower than needed. As of August 2026, only 29 huisarts vacancies were listed on the medical job board (medischebanenbank.nl) — a small number reflecting that most GP practices recruit via practice partnerships/locums rather than posted vacancies, not a true low-demand signal. — [Algemene Rekenkamer](https://www.rekenkamer.nl/webrapport/huisartsen/tekort-aan-huisartsen), [MedNet](https://www.mednet.nl/nieuws/minimaal-750-000-mensen-zoeken-nieuwe-huisarts/), [NTvG vacature board](https://vacatures.ntvg.nl/vacatures/huisarts/)
- Nursing specialist (verpleegkundig specialist) vacancy count: 243 active vacancies found via search aggregation (Indeed/medischebanenbank) as of September 2026. — [Search aggregation, Sept 2026]
- Logistics/transport: UWV lists transport and logistics occupations among those with good job prospects; shortage persists for truck drivers, pilots, bus drivers, and tram operators specifically — general logistics management roles are not singled out as "severe shortage" in the same way as those transport-operator roles. — [UWV Kansrijke beroepen](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/arbeidsmarktinformatie/kansen-beroep/kansrijke-beroepen)
- Volume indicators (posting counts, not unique-vacancy shortage measures — treat as rough demand proxies): Glassdoor/Indeed aggregations in Sept 2026 showed 23,972 "logistics" jobs, 969 "customs" jobs, ~245 "freight forwarding" jobs, and 66 "import export specialist" jobs listed nationally; Indeed.nl showed 900+ "Supply Chain Manager" postings nationally, 200+ "Procurement Manager" postings, and 1,000 "Logistics Manager" postings on LinkedIn NL. — [Glassdoor/Indeed/LinkedIn listings via search, Sept 2026]

### Inferences
- Demand ranking: Registered Nurse and nursing specialist roles = HIGH (structural, multi-year, nationwide, quantified shortage in tens of thousands of vacancies); Medical specialist/doctor = HIGH (explicitly flagged "zeer grote krapte" in every province); GP = HIGH but structurally supply-constrained (low posted-vacancy count reflects practice-based hiring model, not weak demand — the underlying patient-access shortage is severe); general Logistics/Supply Chain/Procurement Manager roles = MEDIUM-HIGH (large posting volumes, steady growth, but not flagged by UWV as an acute occupation-level shortage the way healthcare and transport-operator roles are); Customs/Freight specialist = MEDIUM (moderate, steady posting volume, not a flagged shortage occupation).
- The contrast between GP vacancy postings (only 29) and the scale of the underlying shortage (hundreds of thousands of patients without a GP) suggests that raw job-board posting counts understate real demand for self-employed/practice-based healthcare roles — a caveat the report writer should flag when using posting counts as a demand proxy.

### Gaps
- No single UWV source in this pass gave a precise numeric shortage/vacancy count specifically for "Supply Chain Manager" or "Procurement Manager" as UWV-classified occupations (UWV's shortage classifications tend to use broader/different occupational categories); the logistics demand assessment above is inferred from posting volumes and UWV's broader transport/logistics commentary rather than a single authoritative shortage statistic.
- Did not obtain a comparative time series (e.g., vacancy count in 2023 vs 2026) for logistics roles to show trend direction; only healthcare had a clear historical comparison (highest since 2011).

## What are the entry barriers for each role (certification/diploma, Dutch language, work permit / highly skilled migrant sponsorship)?

### Takeaway
Healthcare roles (nurse, doctor, pharmacist, and other Article 3/36a BIG professions) require mandatory BIG-register registration, proof of B1-level Dutch across all four skills (listening, reading, speaking, writing), and — for non-EU diplomas — a Nuffic/IDW diploma evaluation plus a "certificate of competence" process that can take months; logistics/supply-chain roles have no equivalent licensing regime, generally accept English as a working language (especially at multinational Rotterdam/Schiphip-area logistics firms), and rely mainly on the general Dutch immigration system, most commonly the Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) route with 2026 minimum gross monthly salary thresholds of €5,942 (age 30+) or €4,357 (under 30), or €3,122 for recent graduates of Dutch institutions.

### Cited Findings
- BIG-register (Beroepen in de Individuele Gezondheidszorg) is the mandatory Dutch registry: physicians and other regulated healthcare professionals are required by law to register before practicing or using their protected professional title. — [Wikipedia BIG-register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIG-register), [BIG-register official](https://www.bigregister.nl/registratie)
- Regulated ("Article 3 and 36a") professions requiring BIG registration after a certificate of competence include: pharmacist, doctor, physiotherapist, health psychologist, remedial educationalist, physician assistant, psychotherapist, dentist, nurse, and midwife. — [BIG-register via search](https://english.bigregister.nl/foreign-diploma/procedures/certificate-of-competence/with-a-recognised-qualification)
- Non-EU diploma holders must first validate their diploma with Nuffic (or IDW) before applying for BIG registration; this evaluation step can take up to four weeks (with the full certificate-of-competence/registration process typically taking considerably longer in practice). EU nursing diplomas can skip the Nuffic validation step. — [EMTG / Medpath via search](https://emtg.nl/blog-en-intro-to-dutch-nurse-registration-part-1-big-register/)
- Applicants must demonstrate Dutch language proficiency at B1 level across all four skill areas (listening, reading, speaking, writing) as part of BIG registration; non-EU applicants must additionally prove no active professional restrictions and no relevant criminal convictions (via a CCPS and CGC). — [Dutch Review / BIG-register via search](https://dutchreview.com/expat/health/everything-you-need-to-know-about-becoming-a-nurse-in-the-netherlands/)
- Pharmacist-specific note: Dutch pharmacy education differs substantially from most foreign pharmacy programs, so foreign-trained pharmacists typically must complete a 3-year Dutch master's in pharmacy (often preceded by a "pre-master") before diploma recognition and BIG registration is possible — a materially heavier barrier than for nurses. Foreign pharmacists without BIG registration have limited practice options in the Netherlands. — [KNMP / BIG-register via search](https://www.knmp.nl/beroepsontwikkeling/registratie-en-herregistratie/buitenlands-gediplomeerden), [BIG-register Q&A](https://www.bigregister.nl/buitenlands-diploma/vraag-en-antwoord/wat-is-er-mogelijk-met-een-apothekersdiploma-zonder-big-registratie)
- Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) 2026 minimum gross monthly salary thresholds (effective 1 Jan 2026, excluding 8% holiday allowance, indexed annually by IND): €5,942/month for migrants aged 30+; €4,357/month for migrants under 30; €3,122/month for graduates of Dutch institutions and "highly educated migrants" of any age; €3,583/month for academic researchers at publicly funded institutions. — [Jobbatical](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/netherlands-highly-skilled-migrant-salary-thresholds-2026), [Visa Atlas](https://visaatlas.org/policy-updates/nl-2026-01-kennismigrant-salary-thresholds), [All About Expats](https://allaboutexpats.nl/salary-thresholds-hsm/)

### Inferences
- Practical implication for someone newly established in the Netherlands: the fastest-to-enter, highest-paying logistics roles (Supply Chain Manager, Procurement Manager) are readily accessible via the standard Highly Skilled Migrant visa route without any professional licensing barrier, and salaries in the €68k+/year range for these roles comfortably clear the 30-and-over HSM salary threshold (€5,942/month × 12 ≈ €71,300/year before holiday pay) — making logistics management a substantially faster path to legal high-paid work in NL than regulated healthcare professions.
- By contrast, every healthcare role in scope (nurse, doctor, pharmacist) requires (a) BIG registration, (b) B1 Dutch fluency, and (c) for non-EU-trained professionals, a diploma-recognition process (Nuffic/CIBG) that can take many months to over a year — meaning that despite acute nationwide shortages and strong pay (especially for medisch specialist), healthcare is not a "quick entry" high-pay option for someone newly arrived without a recognized EU/EEA qualification and Dutch language proficiency already in hand. Pharmacists face the steepest barrier of the healthcare roles researched, given the requirement to complete a full Dutch pharmacy master's degree.
- The severity of the language/registration barrier suggests healthcare demand-side shortages (documented above) coexist with a slow-moving supply-side bottleneck for foreign-trained professionals — i.e., the shortage is not primarily solvable via international recruitment without significant lead time for Dutch-language study and diploma recognition.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether medical specialists or nurses commonly qualify for or use the Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) sponsorship route in addition to/instead of the BIG-registration pathway — these are likely largely independent processes (immigration status vs. professional licensing) but this research pass did not find an explicit source describing how the two interact for foreign healthcare workers specifically (e.g., can someone hold an HSM work permit while completing BIG registration, or is a different visa category typically used for regulated healthcare professionals). Flagged as an open question for a follow-up search on "kennismigrant zorgpersoneel BIG registratie" or similar.
- Did not find a definitive statement on whether Dutch B1 language proficiency is formally waived or reduced for any EU-qualified healthcare professionals working temporarily under EU free-movement/mutual-recognition rules versus those seeking full BIG registration for permanent practice — the sources found describe the non-EU pathway most explicitly.
- No explicit language-requirement data was found for logistics/supply-chain roles; it is reasonable to infer (based on general knowledge of the sector and the presence of many international logistics multinationals — CEVA, DHL, Kuehne+Nagel — headquartered/operating around Rotterdam and Schiphol) that English is commonly sufficient for many logistics roles, but this was not directly confirmed by a cited source in this research pass and should be treated as an inference, not a verified fact.
