## What do offshore oil & gas rope access technicians earn in the UK North Sea sector (Aberdeen)?

North Sea offshore rope access work (Aberdeen-based, on installations/platforms) pays substantially above onshore/general work, with day/hourly rates plus allowances, typically on 3-weeks-on/3-weeks-off (or similar 2/2, 3/3) rotations; top-end IRATA Level 3 with an inspection (NDT) trade ticket can push into £400–£600/day territory, with annualized equivalents commonly cited at £70,000–£90,000+.

## What do offshore wind rope-access/blade technicians earn in the UK?

UK offshore wind (Dogger Bank, Hornsea, East Anglia, etc.) is a fast-growing market for rope-access blade repair technicians, requiring GWO Basic Safety Training in addition to IRATA certification; day rates for experienced offshore wind rope access/blade specialists are cited around €200–€400+ (cross-European figure, not UK-specific), while UK-specific narrative examples describe a technician progressing to Level 2 and moving into wind turbine work earning around £62,000/year with rotations.

### Cited Findings
- Blade Repair Technician roles use rope access techniques to inspect and repair turbine blades in situ; external inspection or composite repair work requires mastering industrial rope access — via aggregated search results referencing GWO/wind industry training sources
- GWO Basic Safety Training (BST) — five modules: Working at Heights, First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Awareness, and Sea Survival — is required for most offshore wind roles — [South West Maritime Academy](https://www.southwestmaritimeacademy.com/articles/gwo-basic-safety-training-uk-guide/)
- Day rates for experienced offshore wind specialists can range from roughly €200 to €400+, with advanced qualifications (e.g., ART — Advanced Rescue Training, EFA) increasing employability and day rate — via aggregated search results (source appears to be a general European wind-careers site, not UK-specific; currency is Euros, not GBP, so a direct comparison requires conversion and should be treated as an approximate cross-market reference rather than a UK-specific figure)
- A narrative example (from a UK trade-careers article, exact source page not independently verified due to egress block) describes a technician who, after 18 months, upgraded to IRATA Level 2 and transitioned into wind turbine rope access work, then earning £62,000/year including rotation-based pay
- Hornsea 3 (Ørsted, 2.9 GW) is under construction and expected to support ~5,000 construction jobs and 1,200 permanent operations roles once complete in late 2027 — via aggregated search results citing [Wikipedia: Offshore wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_wind_power) and related coverage
- Dogger Bank Wind Farm (world's largest offshore wind farm) and other UK projects (Hornsea, East Anglia) underpin a UK government target of 50 GW of offshore wind by 2030 — via aggregated search results referencing [Dogger Bank Wind Farm — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogger_Bank_Wind_Farm)

### Inferences
- Because UK offshore wind blade/rope-access day rates were only found expressed in Euros (a cross-European figure) rather than GBP, and because the one GBP annual figure found (£62,000) comes from an unverified narrative/anecdotal source, UK-specific offshore wind rope access pay data is noticeably thinner and less well-documented than North Sea oil & gas pay data. The true UK offshore wind rope-access day rate likely sits in a broadly comparable band to North Sea O&G (given similar offshore logistics, GWO/BOSIET-style survival training overhead, and rotation-based work), but this is an inference, not a confirmed figure.
- The scale of upcoming UK offshore wind construction (Dogger Bank, Hornsea 3, East Anglia, 50 GW 2030 target) strongly implies rising demand for rope-access blade technicians as turbines are commissioned and require in-service inspection/repair, even though a precise UK GBP day-rate figure specific to blade rope access was not found.

## What do onshore/general construction, building maintenance, and telecoms rope-access technicians earn?

### Takeaway
Onshore/general building maintenance, construction, and telecoms rope access work forms the lower-paying "bread and butter" end of the UK trade, with entry-level (IRATA Level 1) technicians typically earning £30,000–£40,000/year or £150–£250/day, rising with IRATA Level 2/3 certification and trade skills (welding, painting, NDT, electrical) to roughly £55,000–£78,000/year or £250–£500+/day for senior/supervisory onshore roles; blended UK-wide averages across all sectors sit around £34,600–£49,000/year depending on data source.

### Cited Findings
- Newly certified IRATA Level 1 technicians typically earn £30,000–£40,000/year, or £150–£250/day as freelancers — via aggregated search results citing multiple UK rope-access career pages (Access All Areas, UK Trade Jobs; direct fetch blocked by egress proxy)
- IRATA Level 1 technicians earn roughly £120–£200/day; Level 2 roughly £180–£300/day; Level 3 supervisors roughly £250–£400+/day — via aggregated search results
- An experienced IRATA Level 3-trained rope access technician can earn up to £78,000/year — via aggregated search results (Access All Areas 2026 article, via search snippet)
- Freelance day rates of £180–£220 are common for starters — via aggregated search results
- Onshore roles (as distinct from offshore) are cited at roughly £60,000–£70,000/year in one source, though this figure appears high relative to other onshore figures cited elsewhere (£30k–£55k realistic range) and may reflect a specific well-paid onshore niche (e.g., senior supervisory or specialist NDT roles) rather than typical onshore rope access work — [Access All Areas](https://aaaropeaccess.co.uk/how-much-can-you-really-earn-as-an-irata-rope-access-technician-in-the-uk-in-2026/) (via search snippet) — flagged as an internal inconsistency in that source's own figures, since it also states a "realistic 2026 range is around £30,000–£55,000"
- Glassdoor UK cites average UK rope access technician salary at £34,598/year (~£17/hour), with typical range £27,334 (25th percentile) to £43,793 (75th percentile), as of April 2026 — [Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/rope-access-technician-salary-SRCH_KO0,22.htm)
- Location affects pay: London/South East reportedly pays 10–20% more than North England/Scotland for rope access work; sectors like wind energy, rail, telecoms, and nuclear are said to pay premiums over generic building maintenance work — via aggregated search results
- A realistic blended UK 2026 salary range across the trade (all sectors, all levels) is roughly £30,000–£55,000/year, depending on location, certifications ("tickets"), overtime, employer type, and degree of independence (employed vs. freelance) — [Access All Areas](https://aaaropeaccess.co.uk/how-much-can-you-really-earn-as-an-irata-rope-access-technician-in-2026/) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- General building maintenance, window cleaning, and standard construction rope access work is the lowest-paying segment of the UK market, roughly £25,000–£40,000/year for Level 1–2 technicians, consistent with it being described in the research brief as the "bread and butter" end; specialist add-on skills (NDT inspection, welding, electrical, blade repair) are the main lever that moves a technician from this band up toward £50k–£78k+.
- The UK rope access market shows a clear three-tier pay structure: (1) general onshore/construction/telecoms — lowest, ~£25k–£45k; (2) offshore wind — middle-to-upper, likely comparable to or slightly below oil & gas given growth investment but less mature pay benchmarking; (3) North Sea offshore oil & gas — highest, ~£70k–£90k+ equivalent or £350–£600/day, reflecting the additional offshore certification burden (BOSIET, OGUK medical), harsher logistics, and legacy oil & gas pay culture.

### Gaps
- No telecoms-specific (e.g., mast/tower rope access) day rate or salary figure was found distinctly quoted apart from the general statement that telecoms "pays a premium" over generic building maintenance — no numeric figure specific to telecoms rope access was located.

## Entry barriers: certification and UK visa/work-permit requirements for non-UK/non-EU nationals

### Takeaway
Beyond IRATA certification (Level 1–3), UK offshore work requires sector-specific survival/medical tickets — BOSIET/FOET plus OGUK Medical (and often CA-EBS, MIST) for oil & gas, and GWO Basic Safety Training for wind; for a foreign (non-UK/non-EEA) applicant, the main additional barrier is the UK's post-Brexit Skilled Worker visa system, which as of 22 July 2025 requires jobs to generally be at RQF degree level (level 6) with a general minimum salary threshold of £41,700/year, though several construction trades (bricklayers, roofers, carpenters/joiners, stonemasons, retrofitters, scaffolders per some sources) sit on the lower-threshold Immigration Salary List (≥£33,400/year or the going rate) and/or the Temporary Shortage List for medium-skilled (RQF 3–5) roles — but no source found explicitly confirms "rope access technician" itself appears on either list.

### Cited Findings
- Aberdeen/North Sea rope access job postings commonly require IRATA Level 3, plus offshore survival and medical certifications: BOSIET/FOET, OGUK Medical, CA-EBS, and MIST — via aggregated Indeed/TotalJobs search results, 2026
- GWO Basic Safety Training (BST) — covering Working at Heights, First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Awareness, and Sea Survival modules — is required for most offshore wind industry roles in the UK — [South West Maritime Academy, GWO Basic Safety Training UK Guide](https://www.southwestmaritimeacademy.com/articles/gwo-basic-safety-training-uk-guide/)
- The UK Shortage Occupation List was officially replaced by the Immigration Salary List on 4 April 2024 — via aggregated search results citing [EIN — UK Temporary Shortage List 2025 guidance](https://www.ein.org.uk/blog/uk-temporary-shortage-list-2025-guidance-skilled-workers)
- On 22 July 2025, the required skill level for a new Skilled Worker sponsorship rose to RQF Level 6 (degree level); simultaneously the government published a Temporary Shortage List of medium-skilled jobs (RQF Level 3–5) that can still be sponsored while remaining on that list — via aggregated search results
- The general Skilled Worker minimum salary threshold for new applications from 22 July 2025 onward is £41,700/year, replacing the previous £38,700 threshold; the worker must also be paid at least the "going rate" for their specific SOC code — via aggregated search results citing [Jobbatical, UK Skilled Worker Visa Minimum Salary & Threshold 2026](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-salary-threshold-employer-guide) and [DavidsonMorris, Skilled Worker Visa Minimum Salary 2026](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/skilled-worker-visa-minimum-salary/)
- Construction roles on the Immigration Salary List (ISL) include bricklayers, electricians, plumbers, and scaffolders per one source, while another source specifies bricklayers, roofers, carpenters and joiners, stonemasons, and retrofitters — sources disagree slightly on the exact construction trades list, so this should be treated as approximate pending direct confirmation from gov.uk — [DavidsonMorris, Immigration Salary List](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/immigration-salary-list/); contradicted in trade specifics by [Tarve, UK Immigration Salary List 2026: All 25 Roles](https://tarve.co.uk/blog/uk-immigration-salary-list-2026)
- An ISL applicant must be paid at least £33,400/year and at least the standard going rate for their SOC code — via aggregated search results
- There are 25 occupations total on the current (2026) Immigration Salary List, spanning care, construction trades, fishing and agriculture, laboratory/pharmaceutical technicians, scientists, and selected creative roles — via aggregated search results
- No source found in this research explicitly lists "rope access technician" (or an equivalent SOC code covering it) as appearing on either the Immigration Salary List or the Temporary Shortage List — this was searched for directly and not found

### Inferences
- Because rope access technician is a specialist skill layered on top of an underlying trade (e.g., NDT inspection, painting, welding, scaffolding, blade repair), a foreign applicant's visa eligibility and salary threshold in practice likely depends on which SOC code / underlying trade classification a sponsoring employer uses to classify the role (e.g., "construction and building trades not elsewhere classified," "engineering technician," or "non-destructive testing technician"), rather than there being a single dedicated "rope access technician" SOC code with its own going rate. This is an inference, not a confirmed fact, and should be flagged clearly to the reader as uncertain.
- Given that offshore oil & gas rope access day rates (~£350-£600/day equivalent to roughly £90k-£150k+ annualized if worked full-time, though rotational work means fewer days actually worked per year) plausibly clear the general £41,700 Skilled Worker salary threshold, while lower-paying onshore/general construction rope access roles (£25k-£45k) likely would not clear the general threshold and would need the role to sit on the lower-threshold Immigration Salary List or Temporary Shortage List to be sponsorable — meaning a foreign national's easiest path to a UK rope access sponsorship is likely through a well-paid offshore role (oil & gas or wind) rather than an onshore/general construction rope access job.

## Demand/outlook for each UK sub-market

### Takeaway
The UK oil & gas sector (Aberdeen/North Sea) faces a well-documented, severe workforce decline over the next five years, while offshore wind is positioned for major growth (though not yet growing fast enough to fully offset oil & gas losses); onshore/general construction rope access demand outlook was not directly addressed by sources found, but is presumably more stable/mature given its broad base across ordinary building maintenance.

### Cited Findings
- The UK oil and gas workforce is forecast to fall from 115,000 in 2024 to between 57,000 and 71,000 by the early 2030s, equating to roughly 400 jobs lost every two weeks for five years, per Robert Gordon University (Aberdeen) research — [World Oil, "North Sea oil and gas decline threatens 115,000-job workforce"](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2026/9/22/north-sea-oil-and-gas-decline-threatens-115-000-job-workforce-report-says/), dated 22 September 2026; corroborated by [Splash247](https://splash247.com/uk-could-lose-400-north-sea-jobs-every-two-weeks-university-warns/) and [Insider (Scotland)](https://www.insider.co.uk/news/north-sea-workforce-could-shrink-35327095)
- An estimated 25,000 North Sea jobs have been lost since the last UK general election (July 2024), per the same RGU-linked reporting — via aggregated search results, September 2026
- UK oil and gas activity is declining faster than offshore wind, carbon capture and storage (CCS), and hydrogen are expanding, risking existing skills, investment, and supply-chain capacity in North East Scotland; the "narrowing window of opportunity" for a successful transition may close in under five years — [Deadline News, "Declining offshore job market needs North East renewable growth, RGU warns"](https://www.deadlinenews.co.uk/2026/06/23/declining-offshore-job-market-needs-north-east-renewable-growth-rgu-warns/), dated 23 June 2026
- Under a successful energy transition scenario, the UK's total offshore energy workforce (oil & gas plus renewables combined) could grow by up to 50%, from over 150,000 in 2023 to 225,000 by end of the decade, with renewable jobs eventually outnumbering oil & gas roles — [Windfair, Robert Gordon University report coverage](https://w3.windfair.net/wind-energy/news/45390-robert-gordon-university-aberdeen-energy-job-report-energy-transition-offshore-uk-workforce-fossil-oil-gas-renewables)
- Conversely, if renewables investment does not increase significantly while oil & gas activity keeps declining rapidly, up to 95,000 potential offshore energy jobs could be at risk — same Windfair/RGU source
- Hornsea 3 (2.9 GW, Ørsted) is under construction, expected to support ~5,000 construction jobs and 1,200 permanent operations roles by completion in late 2027 — via aggregated search results
- UK offshore wind capacity ambitions center on a 50 GW by 2030 target, with Dogger Bank (world's largest offshore wind farm) and Hornsea among flagship projects — via aggregated search results referencing Wikipedia pages on Dogger Bank and offshore wind power

### Inferences
- For a rope access technician deciding where to specialize, the data points toward a near-term (next 3-5 years) risk in staying purely oil & gas-focused in the North Sea, given the RGU-documented severe job losses forecast, while building offshore wind-specific credentials (GWO training on top of IRATA) is likely a better long-term hedge given the committed pipeline of major projects (Dogger Bank, Hornsea 3, East Anglia) reaching construction and then requiring ongoing blade/asset inspection and maintenance work.
- The wide range in the RGU-linked projections (57,000-71,000 by early 2030s versus a more optimistic 225,000 combined offshore energy workforce scenario) shows significant uncertainty in the outlook, hinging heavily on the pace of UK government and private investment in the energy transition — this is a genuine, sourced uncertainty rather than a gap, and should be presented to the reader as a real range of scenarios rather than a single forecast.

- No dedicated demand/outlook statistic specific to onshore/general construction, building maintenance, or telecoms rope access work was found — general construction industry outlook commentary was not part of the search results returned, so the outlook for this specific lower-paying "bread and butter" segment remains unaddressed by evidence gathered in this pass.
