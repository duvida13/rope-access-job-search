## What do offshore wind rope-access/blade repair technicians earn in Denmark (DKK/EUR), and how does OEM/operator employment compare to third-party rope-access contractor employment?

### Takeaway
There is no strong, verified Denmark-specific salary breakdown separating "employed directly by Ørsted/Vestas/Siemens Gamesa" from "employed by a third-party rope-access/blade-repair contractor" — this distinction could not be confirmed with hard numbers. General Denmark rope-access-technician salary aggregator data centers around DKK ~400,000–470,000/year (~€54,000–€63,000), while European contractor day-rate data suggests offshore rope-access/blade specialists can earn considerably more on a day-rate basis (€250–€600/day), which would annualize higher than the aggregator figures if worked full-time, but actual utilization (days worked per year) is unconfirmed for Denmark specifically.

## Entry barriers: GWO certification, IRATA/SPRAT on top of GWO, language requirements, and Denmark's Positive List for non-EU/EEA workers

### Takeaway
GWO Basic Safety Training (BST) is confirmed as the near-universal baseline safety certification for wind industry work (onshore and offshore), on top of which IRATA/SPRAT rope-access certification is an additional specialist qualification. English is confirmed as the working/application language at both Ørsted and Vestas for international roles, though Danish is beneficial or required for some Denmark-domestic-facing roles. Denmark's Positive List for Skilled Work (updated 1 January and 1 July 2026) could not be directly confirmed to include "wind turbine technician," "rope access technician," or a clearly matching title — search snippets list occupations like service electricians, electricians, electronics technicians, industrial technicians, and mechanics, but none of the aggregator/secondary sources found explicitly named a wind-turbine or rope-access occupation on the list, and the official nyidanmark.dk source could not be fetched directly to verify.

### Cited Findings
- GWO Basic Safety Training (BST) is described as "the fundamental requirement for anyone seeking a career as a wind turbine technician... required by most employers worldwide," comprising five core modules (First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Awareness, Working at Heights, and — for offshore — Sea Survival), each valid for two years — [Global Wind Organisation training standards, via search synthesis referencing globalwindsafety.org](https://www.globalwindsafety.org/trainingstandards/wind)
- Additional offshore-specific certifications commonly required beyond GWO BST include BOSIET (offshore survival) and medical fitness clearances — [search synthesis citing GWO/industry training guides]
- As of 2024, "over 190,000 technicians worldwide" hold valid GWO certification, delivered via "over 500 certified providers in more than 50 countries," indicating GWO is a globally standardized, widely accessible certification (i.e., not a Denmark-specific barrier) — [Global Wind Organisation, cited via search synthesis]
- Ørsted "recommends applying in English, as they're an international company and most job advertisements are in English," and states applying in a candidate's native language is also acceptable — [Ørsted Careers, Our Hiring Process](https://orsted.com/en/careers/hiring-process)
- At Vestas, language requirements vary by role: some Denmark-based roles (e.g., a Lead Policy Specialist position) require Danish fluency plus advanced English, while more generally "English is the primary language for international projects at Vestas, with Danish B1-B2 beneficial for Danish projects" — [Vestas Careers job listing](https://careers.vestas.com/job/Copenhagen-S-Lead-Policy-Specialist,-Public-Affairs-Denmark-Regi-2300/1395395633/), [search synthesis]
- Denmark's Positive Lists (for Higher Education and for Skilled Work) are updated twice yearly, 1 January and 1 July; the most recent confirmed update referenced is 1 July 2026, with "164 job titles" on the Higher Education list and "63 job titles" on the Skilled Work list — [nyidanmark.dk news pages, cited via search synthesis: "The Positive Lists are updated as of 1 July 2026"](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/News-Front-Page/2026/06/The-Positive-Lists-are-updated-as-of-1-July-2026) (page itself blocked from direct fetch; figures come from search-engine summary only, not independently verified against the primary document)
- Occupations reported (via secondary/aggregator sources, not the primary nyidanmark.dk list itself) on the Skilled Work Positive List include: "computer technicians, production technicians, service electricians, electricians, electronics technicians, aircraft technicians, plumbers, bricklayers, carpenters, welders, industrial technicians, mechanics, social and health care workers, paramedics, butchers and bakers" — [search synthesis citing nyidanmark.dk / secondary immigration-advisory sites such as Fragomen, pkalert.com, visasupdate.com — none independently fetched]
- One secondary source suggested adjacent-but-not-identical occupations that could conceivably cover wind-sector work: "Service Technician (Iron & Metal) positions can fix machines in factories and wind turbines," "Electricity Distribution Technician is needed for new electricity systems," and "Energy Engineers work on wind power at sea" — but this framing appears to be the search-engine's own inferential synthesis rather than a direct quote of an official list entry, and should be treated as unconfirmed — [search synthesis, sources not independently verified]
- The Positive List for Skilled Work scheme's general mechanism: "if you have been offered a job included in the Positive List for Skilled Work, you can apply for a Danish residence and work permit based on this scheme" — [nyidanmark.dk, cited via search synthesis referencing the official "Positive List for Skilled Work" page](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Work/The-Positive-Lists/Positive-List-for-skilled-work) (page blocked from direct fetch, not independently verified)

### Inferences
- GWO certification is not a meaningful barrier to entry for a foreign rope-access technician targeting Denmark, since it is a globally standardized qualification obtainable in most countries.
- IRATA/SPRAT certification, being an internationally recognized and portable qualification (not Denmark-specific), is also not itself a barrier to entering the Danish market — the real gating factors appear to be (a) whether a specific job title maps onto the Positive List for a non-EU/EEA work permit, and (b) general EU/EEA freedom-of-movement status, which would make the Positive List moot for EU/EEA-national rope-access technicians.
- Because no source found explicitly places "wind turbine technician," "rope access technician," or "blade technician" on Denmark's current (2026) Positive List for Skilled Work, a non-EU/EEA rope-access technician likely cannot rely on this fast-track scheme and would probably need to pursue Denmark's alternative work-permit pathways (e.g., the Pay Limit Scheme, which requires meeting a minimum salary threshold, or a Danish/EU-recognized job offer under general labor-market rules) — this is an inference, not a confirmed fact, since the primary Positive List document could not be checked line-by-line.

## Demand/outlook: Denmark's offshore wind buildout pipeline and whether rope-access/blade-technician roles are in shortage

### Takeaway
Denmark has an ambitious offshore wind buildout pipeline (targeting 14 GW by 2030, up to 52 GW by 2050, including new "energy islands"), which points to strong underlying demand for wind maintenance labor generally, but no source found explicitly confirms a documented shortage specifically in rope-access or blade-repair technician roles (as opposed to wind technicians broadly, or other offshore trades like cable layers and ROV pilots, which one source did flag as in high demand).

### Cited Findings
- Denmark is targeting 14 GW of offshore wind by 2030 and 52 GW by 2050 — [search synthesis citing Danish Energy Agency (ens.dk) coverage, "Offshore wind farms tendered towards 2030"](https://ens.dk/en/energy-sources/offshore-wind-farms-tendered-towards-2030)
- "A procurement procedure will enable establishment of a minimum of 6 GW offshore wind power, to be completed in 2030," spread across sites including North Sea I, Kattegat, Kriegers Flak II, and Hesselø — [Danish Energy Agency, "Denmark's largest tendering procedure for offshore wind power is launched"](https://ens.dk/en/press/denmarks-largest-tendering-procedure-offshore-wind-power-launched)
- The Danish government is "prepared to introduce state support to facilitate the development of 2-3 GW of new offshore wind capacity, with the tender expected to have a bidding deadline in spring 2026," following the cancellation of an earlier failed 3 GW tender in early 2025 — [S&P Global, "Denmark scraps 3 GW offshore wind auction for redesign"](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/013125-denmark-scraps-3-gw-offshore-wind-auction-for-redesign-accelerates-hydrogen-exports)
- Denmark plans two "energy islands," aiming to connect 5-6 GW of offshore wind by 2030 "at the latest," with the Bornholm Energy Island alone designed to collect up to 3-3.8 GW from the Baltic Sea, "doubling the country's current offshore wind capacity" — [stateofgreen.com, "This is what the world's first energy island may look like"](https://stateofgreen.com/en/news/this-is-what-the-worlds-first-energy-island-may-look-like/)
- RWE reports the first turbine is now up at what it describes as Denmark's largest offshore wind farm (dated March 2026), indicating active, ongoing construction (which typically precedes long-term O&M/blade-maintenance labor demand) — [RWE press release, "First turbine up at Denmark's largest offshore wind farm"](https://www.rwe.com/en/press/rwe-offshore-wind-gmbh/2026-03-04-first-turbine-up-at-denmarks-largest-offshore-wind-farm/)
- A general 2026 offshore-careers blog states that "Cable Layers & ROV Pilots Are in High Demand" in offshore wind careers for 2026, implying a broader industry labor crunch, though this source does not specifically name rope-access/blade technicians as a shortage category — [Crewbase, Offshore Wind Careers 2026](https://crewbase.pro/blog/2026/02/offshore-wind-careers-2026)
- Vestas CEO Henrik Andersen was quoted welcoming the Danish government's cancellation/redesign of underperforming offshore tenders, saying "It is right to cancel tenders for offshore wind projects with terms that are not up to date," suggesting industry-side concern about project economics affecting the pace of buildout (which could in turn affect hiring pace/timing) — [search synthesis, S&P Global coverage]

### Inferences
- Denmark's large and growing offshore wind fleet (existing plus a substantial construction/tender pipeline through 2030 and beyond) implies a growing base of turbines requiring ongoing blade inspection and repair over their operational lifetimes, which should structurally support demand for rope-access/blade technicians in Denmark for years to come — but this is an inference from capacity-pipeline data, not a direct labor-market shortage statistic.
- The presence of multiple active third-party rope-access/blade-repair contractors already working in the Danish/Danish-adjacent market (Swire Renewable Energy, Global Wind Service, RTS Wind, Sky-Work Aps, RT9) suggests an established competitive contractor ecosystem rather than a single-employer or thin market — a positive signal for market accessibility, though not proof of "shortage."

This would need to come from an industry association report (e.g., WindEurope, Danish Wind Industry Association / Wind Denmark) which was not searched in this session due to tool-call budget constraints.
