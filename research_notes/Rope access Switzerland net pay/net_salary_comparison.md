## Swiss mandatory payroll deductions (AHV/ALV/BVG) for a blue-collar earner

### Takeaway
The three mandatory social-insurance payroll deductions (AHV/IV/EO, ALV, BVG) are well-documented for 2026 and together take roughly 8–9% of gross salary at CHF 55,000–65,000/year for a younger worker; they do **not** vary by canton. The Quellensteuer (withholding tax) rate, which does vary hugely by canton, could not be confirmed from an official tariff table in this session due to access restrictions — only rough magnitude indicators were found via search snippets.

### Cited Findings
- AHV/IV/EO (old-age, disability, loss-of-earnings insurance) total contribution rate for 2026 is unchanged at 10.6%, split equally: employee pays 5.30%, employer pays 5.30%, applied to the entire gross salary with no cap. — [Lenz Treuhand: Sozialversicherungen 2026](https://www.lenz-treuhand.ch/en/news-insights/sozialversicherungen-2026-ahv-bvg-eo-aenderungen-im-ueberblick/); [Pfeffersack: AHV-Beiträge 2026](https://pfeffersack.ch/lexikon/ahv-beitraege)
- ALV (unemployment insurance) total rate is 2.20%, split equally (employee 1.10%, employer 1.10%), applied up to an AHV annual salary of CHF 148,200 (well above the CHF 55,000–65,000 range in question, so the full 1.10% applies to all of it). — [Swissmem: Sozialversicherungsbeiträge 2026](https://www.swissmem.ch/de/wissen/personalwesen/arbeitsrecht/sozialversicherungsbeitraege-2026.html)
- BVG (mandatory occupational pension) 2026 parameters: entry threshold CHF 22,680/year; coordination deduction CHF 26,460/year; maximum insured salary CHF 90,720/year. The "coordinated salary" (insured salary subject to BVG contributions) = gross salary minus the coordination deduction. — [Lenz Treuhand: Sozialversicherungen 2026](https://www.lenz-treuhand.ch/en/news-insights/sozialversicherungen-2026-ahv-bvg-eo-aenderungen-im-ueberblick/)
- BVG contribution rates are age-banded (standard LPP/BVG minimum schedule, split 50/50 employer/employee in most pension funds): roughly 7% of coordinated salary for ages 25–34, 10% for 35–44, 15% for 45–54, 18% for 55–65 — meaning the **employee** half is approximately 3.5% / 5% / 7.5% / 9% of coordinated salary depending on age band. (This age-banded structure is standard BVG law; exact bracket percentages for 2026 were described consistently across sources but I could not pull the primary BVG law text in this session — treat the age-band numbers as very likely correct but not re-verified against the statute this session.) — [Auditrium: Lohnabzüge in der Schweiz 2026](https://www.auditrium.ch/blog/articles/lohnabzuege-schweiz-aenderungen-2026); [Weka: Lohnabzüge](https://www.weka.ch/themen/personal/lohn-und-gehalt/lohnabrechnung/article/lohnabzuege-die-aktuellen-sozialversicherungsbeitraege/)
- General summary confirms: "the fixed [employee] deductions are AHV/IV/EO (5.3%) and ALV (1.1%). Overall, roughly 12 to 18% of gross salary is deducted [via AHV+ALV+BVG+NBUV etc.], depending on age, salary level, and pension fund." — [Weka: Lohnabzüge](https://www.weka.ch/themen/personal/lohn-und-gehalt/lohnabrechnung/article/lohnabzuege-die-aktuellen-sozialversicherungsbeitraege/)

### Inferences
- For a single blue-collar worker aged roughly 25–34 (a plausible profile for a newcomer to rope-access/construction-type trades), the mandatory AHV+ALV+BVG deduction at **CHF 55,000 gross** is approximately: AHV/IV/EO 5.3% × 55,000 = CHF 2,915; ALV 1.1% × 55,000 = CHF 605; BVG employee ≈3.5% × (55,000 − 26,460 coordinated) = 3.5% × 28,540 ≈ CHF 999. **Total ≈ CHF 4,519/year (≈8.2% of gross)** — this is the researcher's own calculation combining the sourced rates above, not an official total.
- At **CHF 65,000 gross**: AHV 5.3% × 65,000 = CHF 3,445; ALV 1.1% × 65,000 = CHF 715; BVG 3.5% × (65,000 − 26,460 = 38,540) ≈ CHF 1,349. **Total ≈ CHF 5,509/year (≈8.5% of gross)** — again, researcher's own calculation.
- An older worker (35–44 band, ~5% employee BVG rate) would see roughly CHF 400–600/year more deducted at these income levels; the report writer should note the net figures assume a younger worker and could be modestly lower for someone in their 40s+.

### Gaps
- I could not access an official BVG statute/BSV page in this session (bsv.admin.ch was not directly fetched) to double-check the exact 2026 age-banded percentages; the 7/10/15/18% figures are consistent across secondary sources but not confirmed against a primary government page this session.
- Actual BVG employee/employer split can exceed 50/50 in the employee's favor or disfavor depending on the specific pension fund's regulations (50/50 is only the legal minimum requirement, not a mandate that funds must use exactly 50/50) — real payslips may differ slightly from this estimate.

## Swiss Quellensteuer (withholding tax) rate for Tarifcode A, CHF 55,000–65,000, contrasting cantons

- "At CHF 100,000 gross, roughly CHF 8,000 more net income remains in Zug than in Geneva" per year — again above our target income band but confirms the direction and rough order of magnitude (~8% of gross) of the Zug-vs-Geneva gap at a higher income level. — [Lohncomputer: Der grosse Quellensteuervergleich Schweiz](https://www.lohncomputer.ch/de/blog/lesen/der-grosse-quellensteuervergleich-schweiz-fuer-alle-mit-aufenthaltsausweis-b-125/)
- For Geneva, Tarifcode A0 (single, no children, no church tax), the official 2026 table (tar26GE / Swissdec ELM 5.0 format) shows: below CHF 2,450/month (≈CHF 29,400/year) the rate is 0%; between CHF 2,450 and CHF 5,000/month (≈CHF 29,400–60,000/year) the effective rate rises from 0% up to about 5.5%. — [search-derived summary of ge.ch/ESTV Genève barème 2026](https://www.ge.ch/document/baremes-2026-perception-impot-source) (page itself could not be directly fetched this session)

- Schwyz, alongside Zug and Nidwalden, is repeatedly cited as one of the most tax-favorable cantons for employees, in the same low-tax tier as Zug. — [search-derived summary of Swiss canton tax-ranking commentary]

### Inferences (researcher's own approximation — NOT an official lookup)
- For **CHF 55,000/year (≈CHF 4,583/month) in Geneva/Vaud**, since this falls within the CHF 2,450–5,000/month Geneva bracket that rises from 0% to ~5.5%, and CHF 4,583 is close to the top of that bracket, a plausible estimate is **≈4.5–5.5% effective Quellensteuer**, i.e. roughly CHF 2,475–3,025/year. I use ≈5% (CHF 2,750) as a working figure.
- For **CHF 65,000/year (≈CHF 5,417/month) in Geneva/Vaud**, this is just above the top of the cited bracket (which reaches ~5.5% at CHF 5,000/month), so the effective rate is likely somewhat higher, perhaps **≈6–8%**, i.e. roughly CHF 3,900–5,200/year. I use ≈7% (CHF 4,550) as a working figure. This is a rougher extrapolation than the 55k Geneva figure.
- For **CHF 55,000/year in Zug/Schwyz**, given that CHF 72,000/year (a higher income) shows ~5.5% in Zug, a lower income of CHF 55,000 should show a distinctly lower effective rate under Switzerland's progressive Quellensteuer scale — a plausible estimate is **≈3–4%**, i.e. roughly CHF 1,650–2,200/year. I use ≈3.5% (CHF 1,925).
- For **CHF 65,000/year in Zug/Schwyz**, similarly below the 72k/5.5% reference point but closer to it — a plausible estimate is **≈4–5%**, i.e. roughly CHF 2,600–3,250/year. I use ≈4.5% (CHF 2,925).
- **Vaud** is generally considered a higher-tax canton similar to (though not always identical to) Geneva; I am treating Vaud and Geneva as a single "high-tax" pair per the task's framing, but note Vaud's actual Quellensteuer scale was not separately checked this session and could differ somewhat from Geneva's.

### Takeaway
This confirms the background context given: Swiss basic health insurance (LAMal/KVG) is a flat, community-rated premium per adult, not linked to income, not withheld from payroll, and must be budgeted as a separate out-of-pocket mandatory cost on top of net salary.

### Cited Findings
- Background context already established (treated as given per task instructions): ~CHF 393/month per adult in 2026 for basic mandatory health insurance, paid directly to the insurer, not through payroll. — (per task background, not re-verified this session)

### Inferences

## Dutch mandatory payroll deductions (loonheffing) and net pay at €35,000/€42,000

### Takeaway
The Netherlands combines wage tax and national insurance contributions (AOW/ANW/Wlz) into one "loonheffing" system with three 2026 brackets; combined with the labour tax credit (arbeidskorting) and general tax credit (algemene heffingskorting), a single filer at €35,000–€42,000 keeps roughly 80–83% of gross as loonheffing-net income, before the separate mandatory health-insurance premium and possible zorgtoeslag allowance are applied.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 Dutch wage-tax/national-insurance (loonheffing) brackets for a person below state pension age: 35.75% on income up to €38,883; 37.56% on income between €38,883 and €78,426; 49.50% above €78,426. — [Rekenmaat.nl: Belasting op salaris 2026](https://rekenmaat.nl/belasting-op-salaris-2026); [NettoNu.nl: Bruto netto tabel 2026](https://nettonu.nl/populaire-salarissen)
- Maximum arbeidskorting (labour tax credit) for 2026 is €5,685, built up fully by an income of €45,592, after which it is phased down at 6.51% per euro of additional income until it reaches €0 at €132,920. — [Rekenmaat.nl: Belasting op salaris 2026](https://rekenmaat.nl/belasting-op-salaris-2026)
- Maximum algemene heffingskorting (general tax credit) for 2026 is €3,115 (this credit phases down as income rises above a lower threshold, though the exact 2026 phase-out threshold/rate was not confirmed from a primary source this session). — [Rekenmaat.nl: Belasting op salaris 2026](https://rekenmaat.nl/populaire-salarissen); [NettoNu.nl](https://nettonu.nl/populaire-salarissen)
- Illustrative monthly examples found (assumed full standard tax credits, single filer, exact input assumptions not fully specified by the source): €3,000 gross/month → ≈€2,290 net/month; €3,500 gross/month → ≈€2,470 net/month; €4,000 gross/month → ≈€2,750 net/month; €5,000 gross/month → ≈€3,210 net/month. — [Rekenbuddy.nl / general loonheffing calculator summary](https://rekenbuddy.nl/rekentools/loonheffing-berekenen/)
- The illustrative examples above are monthly figures and it is unclear whether they already include the 8% vakantiegeld (holiday allowance) spread across the year or represent gross/net excluding it — this ambiguity was not resolved this session.

### Inferences (researcher's own calculation, combining the sourced brackets/credits above — NOT an official calculator output)
- **At €35,000/year gross:** Tax before credits (all in the 35.75% bracket) ≈ €12,513. Assuming arbeidskorting close to its near-maximum value at this income (my estimate, since €35,000 is fairly close to the €45,592 full-buildup point) ≈ €5,300–5,685, and algemene heffingskorting partially phased down from €3,115 to roughly €2,400–2,700 (my rough estimate given an assumed phase-out starting somewhat above €24,000, an assumption not confirmed for 2026). Net loonheffing tax ≈ €4,100–4,800. **Estimated net-of-tax income ≈ €30,200–30,900/year.**
- **At €42,000/year gross:** Tax before credits ≈ €13,901 (bracket 1, to €38,883) + €1,171 (bracket 2, €38,883–€42,000) = €15,072. Assuming arbeidskorting at or near its €5,685 maximum (since €42,000 is below the €45,592 full-buildup point), and algemene heffingskorting further phased down to roughly €1,900–2,200 (rough estimate). Net loonheffing tax ≈ €7,100–7,500. **Estimated net-of-tax income ≈ €34,500–34,900/year.**
- These figures are notably higher (i.e., imply lower effective tax) than what the illustrative monthly examples above would suggest when annualized (e.g., €3,500/month × 12 = €42,000 gross → ≈€29,640/year net per that snippet, versus my ≈€34,500–34,900 estimate). **This is a real, unresolved discrepancy** — it likely stems from differing assumptions about the exact algemene heffingskorting phase-out schedule and/or whether vakantiegeld/8% holiday pay is being added or already included differently between my calculation and the snippet source. The report writer should treat the true 2026 net figure at €35k/€42k as uncertain within roughly a €2,000–4,000/year band and ideally verify with an official/reputable calculator (nettonu.nl, salaryaftertax.com, or belastingdienst.nl loonheffingstabellen) rather than trusting either estimate alone.

### Gaps
- The exact 2026 algemene heffingskorting phase-out threshold and rate were not confirmed from a primary Belastingdienst source this session (belastingdienst.nl was not directly fetched; access to nettonu.nl, salaryaftertax.com, and other Dutch net-salary calculators was blocked by the network egress proxy).
- It is unclear whether the illustrative "€X gross/month → €Y net/month" examples found via search include the 8% vakantiegeld or not, which materially affects any annualization.
- Recommend the report writer or user directly query an official/reputable Dutch net-salary calculator (e.g., https://salaryaftertax.com/salary-calculator/netherlands or https://nettonu.nl/) for €35,000 and €42,000 gross, single filer, no children, 2026, to get an authoritative figure — this session could not reach those tools.

## Dutch mandatory health insurance premium and zorgtoeslag at these income levels

### Takeaway
The average Dutch basic health-insurance premium for 2026 is about €159/month (≈€1,912/year); a single person qualifies for a partial zorgtoeslag (healthcare allowance) at €35,000 income but not at €42,000, since the 2026 zorgtoeslag income cap for a single person is €40,857.

### Cited Findings
- Average basic health insurance premium for 2026 is €159.30/month, up only €0.58 (+0.37%) from 2025; premiums range from €142.40/month (cheapest) to €185.00/month (most expensive) across roughly 65 basic policies. — [Zorgwijzer: Zorgverzekering 2026 – alle premies bekend](https://www.zorgwijzer.nl/zorgverzekering-2026/zorgverzekering-2026-alle-premies-bekend-overzicht)
- 2026 zorgtoeslag (healthcare allowance) for a single person: maximum ≈€129/month (≈€1,548/year), available in full up to an income of about €29,500/year, phasing down on a non-linear sliding scale to zero at the income cap. — [Man-Man.nl: Zoveel zorgtoeslag krijg jij met jouw inkomen in 2026](https://www.man-man.nl/zoveel-zorgtoeslag-bruto-inkomen-2026/); [Zorgwijzer FAQ: Zorgtoeslag 2026](https://www.zorgwijzer.nl/faq/zorgtoeslag)
- 2026 zorgtoeslag income cap for a single person is €40,857/year — above this, no zorgtoeslag is payable at all. — [Overstappen.nl: Zorgtoeslag 2026](https://www.overstappen.nl/zorgverzekering/zorgtoeslag/); [Belastingdienst / Dienst Toeslagen: Hoeveel inkomen mag ik hebben voor zorgtoeslag?](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/zorgtoeslag/content/maximaal-inkomen-voor-zorgtoeslag) (page referenced via search, not directly fetched this session)
- The maximum zorgtoeslag for a single person is decreasing by about €2/month in 2026 versus 2025, though the income eligibility thresholds are being widened somewhat. — [Zorgwijzer: Zorgtoeslag omlaag in 2026](https://www.zorgwijzer.nl/zorgverzekering-2026/zorgtoeslag-omlaag-in-2026-bereken-hier-hoeveel-je-per-maand-krijgt)

### Inferences (researcher's own approximation)
- At **€35,000/year**, income is between the €29,500 full-allowance threshold and the €40,857 cutoff. Linearly interpolating (a simplification — the real Dutch formula is not strictly linear) the reduction fraction ≈ (35,000−29,500)/(40,857−29,500) ≈ 48%, giving an estimated zorgtoeslag of roughly **€67/month (≈€800/year)** — a rough approximation, not an official calculation.
- At **€42,000/year**, income exceeds the €40,857 cap, so **zorgtoeslag = €0**.
- Net annual mandatory health-insurance cost after allowance: at €35,000 ≈ €1,912 − €800 ≈ **€1,112/year net cost**; at €42,000 ≈ **€1,912/year net cost** (no offset).

### Gaps
- The exact 2026 zorgtoeslag phase-out formula is non-linear per multiple sources, so the €35,000 estimate above (linear interpolation) should be treated as a rough approximation only; an official Belastingdienst/Dienst Toeslagen calculator would give an exact figure (not accessed this session due to tool-call budget/egress restrictions).
