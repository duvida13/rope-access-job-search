# Netherlands Blue-Collar / Skilled-Trade Wages (2026)

## What do specific blue-collar/trade roles pay in the Netherlands (CAO minimums, annual/monthly/hourly gross EUR)?

### Takeaway
Dutch blue-collar wages are set by binding sector CAOs on top of the statutory minimum wage (WML, €14.99/hour gross for age 21+ as of 1 July 2026); most trade roles pay €15–€24 gross/hour (roughly €2,470–€4,150 gross/month at 36–40h/week, i.e. roughly €30,000–€50,000 gross/year), with technical/installation trades (electrician, plumber) reaching higher scale ceilings (up to ~€32/hour, €4,000-€4,500/month) as experience grows.

### Cited Findings
- **Construction (CAO Bouw & Infra, effective 1 Jan 2026)**: gross hourly wage for site workers (bouwplaatswerknemer) aged 21+ ranges €18.95–€23.78/hour, i.e. €3,297–€4,138 gross/month at 40h/week. Function-group breakdown: Construction helper (group A) €18.95/h, €3,297/month; Bricklayer II / street-maker II (group B) €20.02/h, €3,483/month; Skilled GWW worker (group C) €21.24/h, €3,696/month; Bricklayer I / street-maker I (group D) €22.68/h, €3,946/month. From 1 Jan 2026, workers aged 20+ with an MBO-3/MBO-4 diploma count as "vakvolwassen" (fully skilled) and get full CAO wage rather than a youth scale. — [Flexpedia CAO Bouw salarisschalen 2025-2027](https://www.flexpedia.nl/kennis/branches/cao-bouw/salarisschalen/); [FNV Cao Bouw en Infra salarisschalen 2026](https://www.fnv.nl/cao-sector/bouwen-wonen/bouw-infra/verlof/salarisschalen-bouw-en-infra); [TBBouwInfra loontabellen PDF](https://www.tbbouwinfra.nl/Assets/uploads/2025/05/Loontabellen-bouwplaatswerknemer-cao-2025-2027.pdf)
- **Carpenter (timmerman)**: falls under CAO Bouw & Infra's group B (timmerman II, skilled work under supervision) at the same €18.95–€23.78/hour band; separate CAO Timmerindustrie (for prefab/timber-factory workers) had scheduled raises of 3.75% (1 Jul 2025), 2% (1 Jan 2026), 1.75% (1 Jul 2026). — [FNV Cao Bouw salarisschalen](https://www.fnv.nl/cao-sector/bouwen-wonen/bouw-infra/verlof/salarisschalen-bouw-en-infra); [salaris-informatie.nl CAO Timmerfabrieken](https://www.salaris-informatie.nl/cao-sector/timmerfabrieken-timmerindustrie)
- **Electrician / Plumber (CAO Technisch Installatiebedrijf, new agreement 1 Feb 2026 – 31 Jan 2028)**: hourly rates range €15.15–€32.14/hour as of 1 March 2026 across function groups (apprentice through senior technician). Typical electrician gross monthly pay: €2,500–€3,700/month (apprentices/assistants €1,900–€2,400; first-line technician €3,000–€3,700; service technician with on-call/emergency duty up to ~€4,000). Plumber (loodgieter): roughly €2,450–€4,500 gross/month depending on function group/experience. CAO wage increases: +3% on 1 March 2026, one-off €132 gross bonus 1 Nov 2026, structural +€115 gross/month (~3%) on 1 March 2027. — [Techniek Nederland CAO news](https://www.technieknederland.nl/nieuws/cao-technisch-installatiebedrijf-2026-2028-definitief-na-instemming-leden); [demt-flex.nl Elektricien salaris 2026](https://demt-flex.nl/nieuws/elektricien-salaris/); [businesssuite.nl Loodgieter salaris 2026](https://businesssuite.nl/loodgieter-salaris/)
- **Industrial/factory/production worker (productiemedewerker)**: average gross monthly salary cited around €2,750–€2,890/month (Indeed average €2,890/month; other sources €2,390–€2,750/month), full range roughly €2,500–€2,937/month (≈€23,000–€30,000/year gross before shift allowances); shift allowances (ploegentoeslag) commonly add ~€300/month extra. No single national CAO covers all factory work — pay depends on sector CAO (metal, food processing, chemicals, etc.) and varies accordingly. — [Indeed.nl Productiemedewerker salaries](https://nl.indeed.com/career/productiemedewerker/salaries); [Glassdoor.nl Productiemedewerker salarissen](https://www.glassdoor.nl/Salarissen/productiemedewerker-salarissen-SRCH_KO0,19.htm); [Faam.nl salaris productiemedewerker](https://faam.nl/blogs/wat-verdient-een-productiemedewerker-salaris)
- **Warehouse/logistics operative — order picker**: gross hourly wage averages €14.71–€17.50/hour (average ~€15.80/hour), rising to ~€18/hour or more with evening/night/weekend shift surcharges; forklift/reach-truck certification (heftruckcertificaat) adds roughly €1.00–€2.00/hour. Average gross monthly salary ≈€2,762/month (range €2,498–€3,282/month depending on hours/experience/location); Randstad wages run €0.50–€1.50/hour higher than other regions. — [Connectingpeople.nl Salaris Orderpicker 2026](https://www.connectingpeople.nl/salaris/orderpicker/); [Indeed.nl Orderpicker salaries](https://nl.indeed.com/career/orderpicker/salaries); [AXS Logistiek](https://axslogistiek.nl/hoeveel-verdien-je-als-orderpicker-per-uur)
- **Forklift driver (heftruckchauffeur) job-listing data**: postings show gross monthly pay of €2,660–€2,726/month at 40h/week (entry level), €2,600–€3,000/month general range, and up to €2,500–€3,300/month in Rotterdam (≈max €42,900/year gross); some hourly-paid roles list €15.59–€16.96/hour. Some listings reference a scale-4 CAO ceiling of €2,809.99 gross/month full-time. — [Indeed.nl Magazijnmedewerker/Heftruckchauffeur vacatures](https://nl.indeed.com/q-magazijnmedewerker-heftruckchauffeur-vacatures.html)
- **Truck driver**: average Dutch truck-driver gross salary is cited as ~133% of the national statutory minimum wage; employers are adding sign-on bonuses of €1,500–€3,000 and paying for Code 95 (mandatory professional-competence) refresher training to attract drivers amid the shortage. — [gsfleet.io Europe's Fleet Driver Shortage 2026](https://www.gsfleet.io/en/driver-shortage-europe-2026/)
- **Hospitality (CAO Horeca, hotel/restaurant staff)**: statutory minimum wage for 21+ is €14.99 gross/hour as of 1 July 2026 (was €14.71 in H1 2026, +1.9%). CAO Horeca sets 10 salary scales for 2026 ranging €2,468/month (scale I-II, starting salary) to €5,320/month (scale XI, maximum, for the most senior/skilled roles — this ceiling likely applies to supervisory/management-adjacent roles rather than typical blue-collar floor staff). Function groups I+II, III and IV effectively start at the statutory minimum (€14.99/h); groups III–XI received +1% (Jul 2025) and +2.5% (Jan 2026) CAO increases on top. Youth rates: 16-year-old minimum €5.17/hour; 18-year-old €7.50/hour (as of 1 Jul 2026). — [sho-horeca.nl Minimumloon horeca 2026](https://www.sho-horeca.nl/nieuws/minimumloon-horeca-loontabel-2026/); [KHN loontabel 1 juli 2026 PDF](https://static2.khn.nl/public/downloadableFiles/loontabel-1-juli-2026-v2zk.pdf); [salaristabel.nl CAO Horeca 2026](https://salaristabel.nl/salarisschalen/horeca)
- **Agricultural/greenhouse worker (CAO Open Teelten & CAO Glastuinbouw)**: CAO Open Teelten (open-field cultivation, runs 1 Jul 2025–30 Jun 2027) applied a 2.2% wage increase on actual wages from 1 Jan 2026 (function group B tied to statutory minimum wage instead), plus scale C+ hourly increases of ~2% from 1 Jul 2026 and a further 2.2% increase from 1 Jan 2027. CAO Glastuinbouw (greenhouse horticulture, new agreement covers 1 Apr 2026–31 Mar 2027) set increases of 2.15% from 1 Aug 2026 and 1.1% from 1 Jan 2027, with scale B again indexed to statutory minimum wage. Exact absolute €/hour tables were not retrieved in full (behind PDF loontabel documents) — treat these as percentage adjustments on a base tied to statutory minimum wage. — [Werkgeverslijn.nl Loontabel CAO Open Teelten 1-1-2026](https://werkgeverslijn.nl/loontabel-cao-open-teelten-per-1-januari-2026-beschikbaar/); [Werkgeverslijn.nl CAO Glastuinbouw tekst](https://werkgeverslijn.nl/cao-en-lonen/cao-glastuinbouw/); [Opus Recruitment CAO-update agrarisch 2026](https://opusrecruitment.nl/nl/nieuws/cao-verhogingen-per-1-januari-2026/)
- **Cleaner (CAO Schoonmaak- en Glazenwassersbedrijf, agreement covers 2024–2026, wage step 1 Jan 2026)**: +3% general increase plus a flat per-hour add-on (wage group 1: +€0.25/h; group 2: +€0.15/h; groups 3–6: +€0.10/h), applied automatically. Resulting 2026 minimum hourly wages: regular cleaner €15.52–€16.08/hour; specialist cleaning / floor maintenance / team leader €16.70–€17.53/hour; multi-site/multi-team leader €18.20–€19.12/hour. — [Schoonmakend Nederland: nieuwe loontabellen 2026](https://www.schoonmakendnederland.nl/kennisbank/artikel/2025/11/03/cao-loon-gaat-per-1-januari-2026-omhoog-dit-zijn-de-nieuwe-loontabellen); [Facto.nl salaris schoonmakers 2026](https://www.facto.nl/42041/salaris-schoonmakers-stijgt-per-1-januari-2026-dit-zijn-de-nieuwe-uurlonen)
- **Temp/agency work (CAO voor Uitzendkrachten, ABU/NBBU/LBV, new agreement 1 Jan 2026 – 31 Dec 2028)**: this new 3-year CAO replaces the old "inlener-beloning" (client-company pay-matching) system with a system of "equivalent working conditions" — temp workers are entitled to a total package equal in value to permanent staff in comparable roles at the client company, rather than a separately-set agency wage scale. This is highly relevant for blue-collar labor since a large share of Dutch warehouse, agriculture, and production jobs are staffed via temp agencies. Exact new wage tables are published only inside the full CAO PDF (not fully extracted here — see source). — [ABU CAO voor Uitzendkrachten 2026-2028 PDF](https://www.abu.nl/app/uploads/2026/01/CAO-voor-Uitzendkrachten-2026-2028.pdf); [Robert Half: nieuwe ABU-CAO 2026](https://www.robert half.com/nl/nl/inzichten/management-tips/abu-cao-voor-uitzendkrachten)

### Inferences
- Converting to rough annual gross figures (assuming ~40h/week, 12 months, before vacation allowance "vakantiegeld" of +8%, which is legally mandatory on top of these figures in NL): construction general laborer ≈€39,500-€49,700/year; order picker/warehouse ≈€30,000-€39,400/year; cleaner ≈€29,800-€30,900/year (regular grade); electrician journeyman ≈€36,000-€48,000/year; production worker ≈€30,000-€35,000/year. All figures are gross-of-tax and exclude the mandatory 8% holiday allowance, which effectively adds another ~8% to true annual gross compensation.
- Warehouse/logistics and hospitality entry-level wages cluster very close to the statutory minimum wage (€14.99/hour), meaning the CAO functions largely as a floor rather than materially lifting pay above WML for the lowest grades — most of the wage differentiation in these sectors comes from shift/night/weekend surcharges and certifications (e.g., forklift license) rather than base CAO scale.
- Technical trades (electrician, plumber, installation technician) show the widest pay range and highest ceiling among "blue-collar" categories, reflecting formal MBO-level qualification requirements and licensing, effectively bridging toward semi-skilled/technician pay bands.

### Gaps
- Exact current 2026 €/hour tables for CAO Open Teelten and CAO Glastuinbouw by function group were not extracted (only percentage increases found; the actual loontabel PDFs would need to be opened directly for absolute figures).
- Exact current ABU/NBBU temp-worker wage table (absolute €/hour by function group under the new "equivalent working conditions" system) was not extracted — only the structural change was confirmed; the full PDF was not opened.
- No single authoritative average annual gross salary figure (as a national statistic, e.g. from CBS) specifically for "general laborer" or "warehouse operative" occupational codes was retrieved; the figures above come from CAO tables, job-board averages (Indeed/Glassdoor) and recruiter estimates rather than CBS occupation-level wage statistics.

## What is the current demand/labor shortage level for these blue-collar roles?

### Takeaway
The Netherlands has acute, well-documented 2026 shortages in construction/technical trades and in logistics/transport (especially truck drivers), with UWV and sector bodies (TLN, Bouwend Nederland/Techniek Nederland) flagging these as persistent "krapte" (tight labor market) occupations; general warehouse/production and hospitality roles are moderately in-demand but less acute than truck driving and skilled trades.

### Cited Findings
- UWV's 2026 outlook lists transport/logistics roles among the professions in continuing high demand ("kansrijke beroepen"), alongside technical trades. — [iamexpat.nl: UWV reveals most promising professions in the Netherlands for 2026](https://www.iamexpat.nl/career/employment-news/uwv-reveals-most-promising-professions-netherlands-2026)
- The TLN (transport-sector employer body) Barometer, published Q1 2026, estimates the Dutch national truck-driver shortage at 15,000+ open positions and climbing. Demographics are a key driver: many drivers are over 50 and nearing retirement, while few young people enter the profession, partly due to high driving-license costs and the profession's image/working conditions. — [adaption-it.nl Driver shortage 2026: TLN figures](https://www.adaption-it.nl/driver-shortage-2026-what-the-tln-figures-say-and-how-transport-companies-are-keeping-staffing-levels-stable/); [gsfleet.io Europe's Fleet Driver Shortage: 2026 Numbers](https://www.gsfleet.io/en/driver-shortage-europe-2026/)
- Half of European trucking companies report they cannot expand operations due to workforce/driver constraints; Dutch employers are responding with higher base pay, sign-on bonuses (€1,500-€3,000), and paid Code 95 refresher training. — [gsfleet.io Europe's Fleet Driver Shortage: 2026 Numbers](https://www.gsfleet.io/en/driver-shortage-europe-2026/)
- UWV is separately flagging shortages in the logistics industry more broadly (not just driving roles). — [trans.info: Lack of drivers in the Netherlands](https://trans.info/en/lack-of-drivers-in-the-netherlands-every-second-company-has-trouble-finding-workers-79697)
- Wage increases across construction and technical-installation CAOs in 2026 (e.g., the 3% Techniek Nederland increase, described elsewhere as amounting to a reported ~13% multi-year uplift in one trade-press headline for the technical installation sector) reflect employer pressure to retain workers amid shortages in skilled trades. — [Solarmagazine.nl: Nieuwe cao voor technisch installatiebedrijf, lonen 13 procent omhoog](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i38193/nieuwe-cao-voor-technisch-installatiebedrijf-lonen-13-procent-omhoog)

### Inferences
- The combination of an aging workforce (especially in trucking) and continuing wage growth well above general inflation across construction/technical CAOs (3%+ structural raises stacked with one-off bonuses) is consistent with genuine, employer-acknowledged structural labor scarcity in these blue-collar segments, not just cyclical tightness.
- Warehouse/production/hospitality roles appear to be in steady, high-volume demand (thousands of live vacancies per city on Indeed.nl at any time) but without the same degree of acute, publicly quantified shortage figures (e.g., no equivalent "15,000+ open positions" headline number was found for order pickers or hospitality staff specifically).

### Gaps
- No specific UWV shortage-occupation ranking/percentage (e.g., UWV's "spanningsindicator" tightness index) broken out by exact occupation (order picker, cleaner, hospitality staff) was retrieved — only general "kansrijke beroepen" and logistics/technical mentions. A direct UWV.nl krapte-indicator table would sharpen this further.
- No CBS vacancy-rate or unemployment-rate figures specific to blue-collar occupational categories were retrieved in this pass.

## Entry barriers: certification, language, and work-permit rules for blue-collar work

### Takeaway
Blue-collar/trade jobs in the Netherlands are governed by MBO-level vocational qualification norms (foreign diplomas assessed via Nuffic/SBB) and are practically closed to most non-EU/EEA newcomers, because the Highly Skilled Migrant salary thresholds (2026: €5,942/month age 30+, €4,357/month under 30) sit far above typical blue-collar pay, and the general non-EU work-permit route (TWV) requires a 5-week EU/EEA labor-market test that is waived only for shortage-listed or specially-designated roles — meaning EU/EEA nationals (visa-free, no permit needed) remain the realistic labor pool for most blue-collar hiring, while non-EU workers need a specific TWV/shortage-occupation route.

### Cited Findings
- The Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant) visa route requires minimum gross monthly salary thresholds that are set for the 2026 salary year — these thresholds are far above blue-collar wage levels reported above (e.g., construction, warehouse, hospitality roles paying €2,470-€4,150/month gross fall well under both the age-30+ and under-30 kennismigrant thresholds), meaning blue-collar workers cannot qualify for this route regardless of skill level. [Threshold figures per the task brief: €5,942/month age 30+, €4,357/month under 30 — not independently re-verified as a primary source in this pass, but consistent with known kennismigrant salary-criteria structure.]
- For non-EU/EEA nationals without a kennismigrant-qualifying salary, the applicable route is the TWV (Tewerkstellingsvergunning) short-stay work permit (for under 90 days) or the longer GVVA combined permit. A mandatory labor-market test requires the employer to advertise the vacancy to EU/EEA/Swiss candidates for at least 5 weeks before applying to UWV for a TWV. This test is waived only for highly skilled migrants, intra-company transferees (ICT permit), researchers, and roles on the national shortage-occupations list. — [Jobbatical: Netherlands TWV Work Permit 2026 guide](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/netherlands-short-term-work-permit-twv-guide); [dutch-law.com: Work permit in the Netherlands (TWV/GVVA)](https://dutch-law.com/employment-law/work-permit-netherlands.html)
- UWV charges no government fee for the TWV permit itself as of 2026, and employers typically receive a decision within five weeks. — [Jobbatical: Netherlands TWV Work Permit 2026 guide](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/netherlands-short-term-work-permit-twv-guide)
- Construction-sector CAO rules explicitly tie skilled-worker ("vakvolwassen") status and full pay to holding an MBO-3 or MBO-4 diploma from age 20 (as of 1 Jan 2026), underscoring that formal Dutch/EU-recognized vocational qualification level is directly linked to CAO pay grade, not just experience. — [Flexpedia CAO Bouw salarisschalen](https://www.flexpedia.nl/kennis/branches/cao-bouw/salarisschalen/)

### Inferences
- Because most blue-collar CAO wages (roughly €2,470-€4,500/month gross across the sectors surveyed) sit far below the Highly Skilled Migrant thresholds cited in the task brief, non-EU nationals seeking blue-collar work in the Netherlands are not eligible for the "easy" high-skilled visa route used by white-collar/professional migrants — this structurally channels non-EU blue-collar labor into the slower, employer-driven TWV/labor-market-test process, or into shortage-occupation-listed roles (where the 5-week EU test is waived) such as truck driving, given the scale of the documented driver shortage.
- In practice this means the Dutch blue-collar labor market for these roles is realistically open primarily to EU/EEA/Swiss nationals (who need no work permit at all) and existing Netherlands residents, with the non-EU pathway meaningfully narrower and slower than for skilled/professional migration — directly relevant to any side-by-side comparison against Switzerland's blue-collar permit regime.
- Foreign vocational (trade) qualifications for roles like electrician or plumber would need recognition via Nuffic (diploma comparison) and/or SBB (vocational-education recognition body) before an employer/CAO would grant full "vakvolwassen"/skilled pay-grade status, paralleling the MBO-3/4 rule found for construction.

### Gaps
- This pass did not independently re-verify the exact 2026 Highly Skilled Migrant salary thresholds (€5,942/month age 30+, €4,357/month under 30) against a primary IND.nl source — these were taken as given in the task brief and used only for comparative reasoning; a follow-up check against ind.nl is recommended if exact-figure precision is needed for the final report.
- Did not find a specific, current (2026) list of "shortage occupations" (kansrijke/schaarste beroepen) exempted from the TWV labor-market test with an explicit itemization of which blue-collar trades (beyond the general logistics/transport shortage context) are on it — UWV's own shortage-occupation list was not directly retrieved in this pass.
- Language requirements: this pass did not find a specific, sourced breakdown of which blue-collar employers require Dutch vs. tolerate English-only (e.g., specifically for logistics/warehouse employers known to hire many international/EU workers). This is flagged as a gap — the task brief's premise (that logistics/warehouse employers are more English-tolerant) was not independently confirmed with a citation in this research pass.
