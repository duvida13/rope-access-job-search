# Switzerland: Blue-Collar and Skilled-Trade Job Markets (2026)

## What do specific blue-collar/trade roles pay in Switzerland (annual gross CHF, 13th-month structure)?

### Takeaway
Swiss blue-collar wages are set on a monthly-salary basis paid 13 times a year (12 months + a 13th-month salary, either as a lump sum or pro-rated), which is the near-universal norm under GAV/CCT collective agreements. GAV-mandated minimums for trade-qualified (EFZ) workers cluster around CHF 4,500–5,300/month (≈CHF 58,000–69,000/year including 13th month), while unskilled/entry roles bottom out around CHF 3,460–4,747/month (≈CHF 45,000–62,000/year). Actual market averages reported by job portals run somewhat above GAV floors.

### Cited Findings
- **Construction worker (Bauhauptgewerbe, LMV 2026–2031)**: wage class C (unskilled) CHF 4,747–4,885/month (CHF 26.95–27.75/hour) depending on wage zone; wage class B (specialized) CHF 5,184–5,458/month (CHF 29.45–31.00/hour); a new "construction site allowance" (Baustellenzulage) of CHF 4/day applies from 2026 for shifts ≥5.5 hours — [SORBA blog on Baulohn](https://blog.sorba.ch/baulohn-in-der-schweiz-sorba-edv); [LMV 2026 PDF](https://svk-bau.ch/wp-content/uploads/2026_01_20-LMV-2026-2031-DE.pdf)
- Market-average (not just GAV floor) gross annual salary for "Bauarbeiter" in Switzerland: median CHF 63,800/year including 13th month and bonus, based on 339 salary entries (Indeed/jobs.ch aggregation); average hourly CHF 35.91; Zurich-specific average CHF 63,150/year, CHF 33.71/hour — [Indeed career salaries](https://ch.indeed.com/career/bauarbeiter/salaries), [jobs.ch salary tool](https://www.jobs.ch/de/lohn/?canton=ch&term=bauarbeiter)
- Temporary/agency construction workers: CHF 31.32/hour in normal wage zones, CHF 33.44/hour in high-wage zones (Bern agglomeration, Arc Lémanique, Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Geneva, Zurich) — [fachkraft.ch Temporärlohn](https://www.fachkraft.ch/temporaerlohn-schweiz/)
- **Electrician (Elektroinstallateur EFZ)**: GAV minimum CHF 4,500/month right after apprenticeship completion, rising to CHF 5,000/month after one full year of branch experience in Switzerland (as of the following Jan 1); average market gross annual salary ≈ CHF 68,900 — [fachkraft.ch](https://www.fachkraft.ch/elektroinstallateur-lohn-schweiz/)
- **Montage-Elektriker (assembly electrician) EFZ**: GAV minimum CHF 4,300/month right after apprenticeship, CHF 4,700/month after one full year; average gross annual ≈ CHF 65,000 — [fachkraft.ch](https://www.fachkraft.ch/montage-elektriker_lohn/)
- General electrician range: CHF 65,000–110,000/year depending on specialization/seniority — [einwandern-schweiz.ch](https://einwandern-schweiz.ch/arbeiten-in-der-schweiz/elektriker-gehalt-schweiz/)
- **Plumber (Sanitärinstallateur EFZ)**: GAV Gebäudetechnik minimum CHF 4,600–5,300/month gross depending on experience; EBA-qualified installer GAV minimum CHF 4,100–4,500/month. Average market gross annual salary ≈ CHF 68,165 (≈CHF 5,243/month with 13 payments). No 2026 wage increase applied because CPI (LIK) rose only 0.2% by end of August 2025 — [fachkraft.ch Sanitärinstallateur](https://www.fachkraft.ch/sanitaerinstallateur-lohn-schweiz/)
- **Carpenter (Zimmermann)**: covered by GAV, monthly-wage structure (specific 2026 monthly figure not extracted beyond source title) — [fachkraft.ch Zimmermann](https://www.fachkraft.ch/zimmermann-lohn-schweiz/) *(figure not fully captured — see Gaps)*
- **Warehouse/logistics worker (Lagermitarbeiter/Lagerist)**: national average gross annual salary ≈ CHF 60,000/year including bonus/13th month (682 entries); range roughly CHF 18,000–120,000 depending on experience/canton, with a "typical" band of CHF 55,000 (no experience) to CHF 69,500 (experienced); highest-paying industry is Building trade/Real estate (CHF 65,400/year), followed by machine/systems construction and pharma — [jobs.ch salary tool](https://www.jobs.ch/en/salary/?canton=ch&term=lagermitarbeiter); Zurich canton hourly average CHF 29.23/hour (March 2026 data), with postings for cold-storage warehouse work starting at CHF 29.43/hour — [Indeed career salaries Kanton Zürich](https://ch.indeed.com/career/lagermitarbeiter/salaries/Kanton-Z%C3%BCrich)
- **Truck driver (LKW-/Lastwagenfahrer)**: gross annual range CHF 53,711–70,140/year; median CHF 62,400/year (2026); Ticino and Romandie wages run 5–10% below the Swiss average, while Zurich, Basel, and Zug pay median-to-maximum; ADR hazmat/night/weekend allowances add 10–20%; apprenticeship (Strassentransportfachmann/-frau EFZ, 3 years) pays ≈CHF 800/1,000/1,200 per month across the three years — [lohncheck.ch](https://lohncheck.ch/de/lohn/diverses/lkw+-+lastwagen+fahrer|); average chauffeur monthly salary ≈ CHF 4,834–5,000/month, some postings from CHF 29.25/hour gross — [funkyjobs.ch](https://www.funkyjobs.ch/ratgeber/lohn/chauffeur)
- **Hospitality (hotel/restaurant, L-GAV 2026 minimum monthly gross wages)**: no vocational training CHF 3,713/month; no training but with Progresso certificate CHF 3,943/month; Federal Certificate of Competency (EFZ-equivalent) CHF 4,070/month; Federal Diploma CHF 4,528/month; Federal Diploma + further education CHF 4,635/month; Professional Examination holders CHF 5,293/month; interns/trainees CHF 2,390/month. These are gross monthly minimums paid 13x/year under L-GAV. Seasonal contracts get the new rate from start of summer season 2026 (winter season ends by Apr 30, 2026); other contracts from Jan 1, 2026 — [l-gav.ch official announcement](https://l-gav.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/20251020-mi-mindestl%C3%B6hne-2026-l-gav-gastgewerbe-DE.pdf); [GastroSuisse table](https://gastrosuisse.ch/assets/de/recht/l-gav/gastgewerbliche-loehne/gastrosuisse-tabelle-mindestloehne-l-gav-2026.pdf)
- **Agricultural worker**: national reference minimum (Normalarbeitsvertrag Landwirtschaft) gross monthly wage CHF 3,460 as of Jan 1, 2026 (+0.3%); cantons layer additional rules — Basel-Stadt cantonal minimum wage CHF 22.20/hour (applies via NAV reference to national agricultural wage guideline); Geneva, Vaud, Neuchâtel have binding cantonal/NAV wage floors; Valais uses a GAV — [pinus-buchhaltungssoftware.ch](https://pinus-buchhaltungssoftware.ch/news/richtloehne-2026-schweizer-landwirtschaft/); [agrimpuls.ch wage guideline PDF](https://www.agrimpuls.ch/fileadmin/agrimpulsch/Arbeitsrecht/Lohnrichtlinien/Lohnrichtlinie_2026_d.pdf)
- **Harvest worker (Erntehelfer)**: median gross annual salary ≈ CHF 50,000/year (full-time equivalent); range CHF 45,307–61,763/year; hourly rate roughly CHF 18–24/hour following the relevant canton's NAV, with piece-rate (Akkord) supplements boosting income; ~20,000 foreign workers employed in Swiss agriculture, concentrated in vegetable growing; most postings in Romandie (Valais/Vaud viticulture), Lake Constance region (fruit), and Bernese Seeland (vegetables); Graubünden pays best, Lucerne pays least — [lohncheck.ch Erntehelfer](https://lohncheck.ch/de/lohn/diverses/erntehelfer)
- **Cleaner (Reinigungskraft, GAV Reinigungsbranche Deutschschweiz 2026–2029)**: new GAV in force Jan 1, 2026 for ~75,000 employees in German-speaking Switzerland, declared generally binding (covers even 1–5-employee firms). Wage level I hourly minimums rise 3% on Jan 1, 2026, and a further 2.5% on Jan 1, 2028. Monthly minimum for EFZ-qualified "Fachfrau/Fachmann Reinigungstechnik" rises by CHF 200 to CHF 4,700/month (EBA-qualified to CHF 4,200/month), based on a 182-hour reference month. Wage level II (post GAV-course) is now CHF 1.50/hour above level I (was CHF 1.00) — [Syna announcement](https://syna.ch/aktuell/neuer-gav-2026-2029-fuer-die-reinigungsbranche-in-der-deutschschweiz-lohnerhoehung-ab-1-januar-2026); a separate GAV covers French-speaking Switzerland (Romandie) — [gav-service.ch Reinigungssektor Westschweiz](https://gav-service.ch/gav/185002/version/5/vertrag/11526)
- **Industrial/factory/production worker**: average gross monthly wage ≈ CHF 5,500 (national average, manufacturing broadly); average hourly ≈ CHF 26.44–29.83; average gross annual ≈ CHF 62,400–63,151 (2024–2026 range); food processing averages CHF 4,500/month vs. mechanical engineering CHF 6,000/month, showing a strong sub-sector spread; hourly range CHF 22.73–30.76 depending on experience/location — [Indeed career salaries Produktionsmitarbeiter](https://ch.indeed.com/career/produktionsmitarbeiter/salaries)
- Rope-access/height-work technician: no dedicated GAV or Swiss-specific wage data source found in this research pass (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The 13th-month-salary structure is not a bonus but a contractual baseline embedded in GAV minimum tables (monthly figures × 13 = the annual gross figure job seekers should expect), so any CH-vs-NL comparison should convert CH monthly GAV minimums × 13, not × 12.
- Within a given trade, the gap between the legal GAV/NAV floor and the market-average wage reported by portals (jobs.ch/Indeed) is often 15–30%, meaning floor figures understate what employers actually pay for experienced staff, especially in high-demand cantons (ZH, BS, GE).
- Cleaning, hospitality, and agriculture — the three "explicitly protected" GAV/NAV sectors — sit at the bottom of the blue-collar wage ladder (CHF 3,460–4,700/month range), while skilled-trade EFZ roles (electrician, plumber, construction specialist) sit meaningfully higher (CHF 4,500–5,500/month range).

### Gaps
- Could not extract a specific 2026 monthly GAV wage figure for Zimmermann (carpenter) beyond the source's headline; the fachkraft.ch page title suggests such data exists but was not captured in the search snippet.
- No wage or GAV data found specifically for "rope access / height-work technician" (Höhenarbeiter/travailleur en hauteur) as a distinct Swiss occupational category — this appears to be a niche role in Switzerland typically covered under general construction/industrial GAVs rather than a standalone wage table; a targeted follow-up search on IRATA Switzerland or specific rope-access employers would be needed.

## How do wages vary by canton/language region, and what are the binding GAV/NAV minimums?

### Takeaway
Switzerland has both cantonal statutory minimum wages (5 cantons plus the city of Lucerne, mostly in French-speaking Switzerland and Ticino) and sector-specific GAV/NAV wage floors that vary by "wage zone" (high-cost regions such as Zurich, Basel, Geneva, and the Lake Geneva arc pay more than "normal" zones); Ticino and Romandie construction/transport wages tend to sit a few percent below the German-speaking average despite Ticino and Geneva having their own high cantonal minimum wage laws.

### Cited Findings
- Cantonal statutory minimum wages 2026: Geneva CHF 24.59/hour (highest in the world, per source), Basel-Stadt CHF 22.20/hour, Jura CHF 21.40/hour, Neuchâtel CHF 21.35/hour, Ticino CHF 20.00–20.50/hour — [checkeverything.ch Mindestlohn Schweiz 2026](https://checkeverything.ch/de/blog/mindestlohn-schweiz-2026)
- Basel-Stadt's cantonal minimum wage (CHF 22.20/hour from Jan 1, 2026) is explicitly cross-referenced into the agricultural NAV for the canton — [bs.ch FAQ Mindestlohn](https://www.bs.ch/wsu/awa/arbeitsbeziehungen/loehne/mindestlohn/haeufige-fragen-zum-kantonalen-mindestlohn-faq)
- LMV construction wage zones: Geneva, Vaud, and the Basel region form one (higher) wage zone; Ticino forms its own separate zone; canton Bern's wage zones were harmonized so all wage classes now use the same ("Blue") zone — [einwandern-schweiz.ch Mindestlohn](https://einwandern-schweiz.ch/arbeiten-in-der-schweiz/mindestlohn-schweiz/); [SORBA blog LMV 2026](https://blog.sorba.ch/lmv-2026)
- Construction class-C minimum ranges CHF 4,747–4,885/month and class-B CHF 5,184–5,458/month specifically because of these zone differences — [blog.sorba.ch](https://blog.sorba.ch/baulohn-in-der-schweiz-sorba-edv)
- Temporary/agency construction: CHF 31.32/hour (normal zones) vs. CHF 33.44/hour (high-wage zones: Bern agglomeration, Arc Lémanique, Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Geneva, Zurich) — [fachkraft.ch](https://www.fachkraft.ch/temporaerlohn-schweiz/)
- Truck driver wages: Ticino and Romandie run 5–10% below the Swiss average; Zurich, Basel, and Zug pay at or above the median — [lohncheck.ch LKW](https://lohncheck.ch/de/lohn/diverses/lkw+-+lastwagen+fahrer)
- Cleaning GAV: separate collective agreements exist for German-speaking Switzerland (2026–2029, generally binding, ~75,000 employees) and for the Romandie ("Westschweiz") — figures given above apply to the Deutschschweiz GAV; Romandie GAV wage levels were not extracted in this pass — [Syna](https://syna.ch/aktuell/neuer-gav-2026-2029-fuer-die-reinigungsbranche-in-der-deutschschweiz-lohnerhoehung-ab-1-januar-2026); [gav-service.ch Westschweiz](https://gav-service.ch/gav/185002/version/5/vertrag/11526)
- Agriculture: cantonal minimum wages (e.g., Neuchâtel) and binding NAV wage rules (Vaud, Geneva) or GAV (Valais) must be respected on top of the CHF 3,460/month national reference guideline — [pinus-buchhaltungssoftware.ch](https://pinus-buchhaltungssoftware.ch/news/richtloehne-2026-schweizer-landwirtschaft/)
- Harvest-worker pay is highest in Graubünden and lowest in Lucerne, with the largest job volumes in Romandie viticulture, Lake Constance fruit-growing, and Bernese Seeland vegetable-growing — [lohncheck.ch Erntehelfer](https://lohncheck.ch/de/lohn/diverses/erntehelfer)

### Inferences
- The French-speaking cantons (Geneva, Neuchâtel, Jura) and Ticino have leaned toward statutory cantonal minimum-wage laws (a policy tool largely absent in German-speaking Switzerland), which paradoxically gives some Romandie/Ticino workers a higher wage floor by law even though sector-specific market wages (e.g., trucking, construction wage zones) in French-speaking Switzerland and Ticino often run at or below the German-speaking Swiss average — the wage floor and the market wage move somewhat independently across the language border.
- Zurich, Basel, and Geneva/Vaud consistently appear as the highest-paying cantons across nearly every blue-collar trade examined, consistent with their higher cost of living.

### Gaps
- No specific Romandie ("Westschweiz") cleaning-sector GAV wage table was retrieved (only the Deutschschweiz figures were captured in detail).
- No canton-by-canton breakdown found for electrician, plumber, or carpenter GAV wages (their GAV wage tables appear to be set nationally by the Gebäudetechnik/Elektro branch GAVs rather than zoned by canton, unlike the construction LMV) — this should be treated as an inference, not confirmed.

## What is current labor demand/shortage level for these blue-collar roles?

### Takeaway
The overall Swiss skilled-worker shortage has narrowed sharply (from many occupational groups to a handful), but construction (site/production management roles) and healthcare remain the two clear shortage sectors as of 2025–2026; broader blue-collar segments (general warehouse, general hospitality staff) are described as closer to balanced.

### Cited Findings
- In 2025 only 4 of 32 tracked occupational groups showed a skilled-worker shortage, down from many more in prior years, per the Swiss labor-market shortage index — [stellenmarktmonitor.uzh.ch Fachkräftemangel-Index](https://www.stellenmarktmonitor.uzh.ch/de/indices/fachkraeftemangel.html)
- Healthcare specialists (doctors, nursing professionals, physiotherapists) have held the #1 shortage ranking since 2022, driven by an aging population — [ki-blick.ch](https://www.ki-blick.ch/blog/fachkraeftemangel-schweiz-2026)
- Construction managers, foremen, and production leaders (site managers, painting foremen, machine-building leaders) rank #2 in shortage severity, driven by a robust construction order backlog; the Swiss Building Masters Association (Baumeisterverband) reported only a 78% apprenticeship-placement rate for 2025, implying a widening future skills gap at the trained-worker level — [ki-blick.ch](https://www.ki-blick.ch/blog/fachkraeftemangel-schweiz-2026)
- Demographic pressure: Switzerland is projected to lose ~90,000 employed persons/year to retirement between 2025–2030 while only ~75,000 young people enter the workforce annually, a structural gap that will likely widen shortages across many blue-collar trades over time — [ki-blick.ch](https://www.ki-blick.ch/blog/fachkraeftemangel-schweiz-2026)
- No dedicated hospitality-sector shortage statistic was returned in this search pass despite hospitality being widely discussed anecdotally as short-staffed; source acknowledges data gap directly ("search results don't provide specific data on Gastgewerbe") — [ki-blick.ch summary](https://www.ki-blick.ch/blog/fachkraeftemangel-schweiz-2026)

### Inferences
- Given the shrinking headline shortage-occupation list but the persistence of construction and healthcare, employer demand pressure for general "unskilled" blue-collar labor (warehouse, cleaning, generic hospitality) is likely closer to balanced/moderate rather than acute, while skilled-trade construction supervisory and health-adjacent care roles face genuine structural shortages.

### Gaps
- No hospitality-specific, agriculture-specific, or logistics-specific shortage index figures were found in this pass; a follow-up search directly on GastroSuisse or hotelleriesuisse shortage reporting, and on SECO's sector vacancy statistics, would sharpen this.

## Entry barriers: vocational certification, language, and work-permit rules for blue-collar work

### Takeaway
Foreign trade qualifications require formal SBFI recognition (or validation of 5+ years' experience) to be treated as equivalent to a Swiss EFZ/EBA, and non-EU/EFTA (third-country) nationals face a materially harder path into blue-collar work than into "highly qualified" white-collar roles: several blue-collar sectors (construction, hospitality, cleaning, security, travel) require a work permit in hand before starting work, and employers must clear a "domestic preference" (Swiss/EU labor market priority) test plus prove local wage/working-condition compliance for any third-country hire.

### Cited Findings
- Foreign vocational qualifications comparable to a Swiss EFZ (Fähigkeitszeugnis) or EBA (Berufsattest) require a formal equivalence decision from SBFI (State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation); the portal anerkennung.swiss is the central starting point to determine whether recognition is required and which authority handles it — [SBFI Ausländische Berufsqualifikationen](https://www.sbfi.admin.ch/de/auslaendische-berufsqualifikationen-in-der-schweiz); [SBFI Anerkennung von Berufsqualifikationen](https://www.sbfi.admin.ch/de/anerkennung-von-berufsqualifikationen)
- Adults with at least 5 years of relevant professional experience can have their practical competencies validated ("Validierung von Bildungsleistungen") to obtain an EFZ or EBA without going through a full formal training program — an alternative path for foreign tradespeople without a directly recognized diploma — [berufsbildung.ch lexicon](https://www.berufsbildung.ch/de/lexikon/anerkennung-gleichwertigkeit-auslaendischer-abschluesse)
- For 2026, the Federal Council decided not to activate the "valve clause" (Ventilklausel) contingent restriction, because overall immigration figures stayed below statutory thresholds — but this does not remove the underlying quota/contingent system for third-country nationals, which remains a dual system (separate cantonal and federal annual quotas) — [recruitplus.ch Drittstaaten-Bewilligungen](https://recruitplus.ch/blog/bewilligungen-drittstaaten-arbeitsmarkt-ch)
- In construction (main and secondary), hospitality, cleaning services (in businesses and private households), security/surveillance services, the travel industry, and the erotic industry, a work permit must be obtained BEFORE starting any work — unlike other sectors, where a "report from day one" rule applies instead. This is a materially stricter rule specific to blue-collar-heavy sectors — [SEM Weisungen VFP, cited via search summary](https://www.sem.admin.ch/dam/sem/de/data/rechtsgrundlagen/weisungen/fza/weisungen-fza-d.pdf.download.pdf/weisungen-fza-d.pdf)
- To maximize approval odds for a third-country hire, employers must: (1) demonstrate compliance with "domestic preference" (proving no suitable Swiss/EU/EFTA candidate was available), (2) prove the foreign candidate's necessary qualifications, and (3) apply local- and industry-standard wage and working conditions (i.e., meet or exceed the relevant GAV/NAV wage floor) — [recruitplus.ch](https://recruitplus.ch/blog/bewilligungen-drittstaaten-arbeitsmarkt-ch)

### Inferences
- Because "highly qualified" is an explicit legal filter cantonal/federal migration authorities apply favorably to white-collar/specialist roles, and because several blue-collar sectors additionally require the permit to be secured before the first day of work (rather than the more lenient "report within 8 days" rule available elsewhere), third-country blue-collar candidates face a compounded barrier: they must clear the general quota/domestic-preference test AND cannot start provisionally while paperwork finishes, unlike some other sectors.
- Language requirements were not directly quantified in sourced material during this pass, but the client-facing nature of hospitality and the safety-critical nature of construction/trades strongly imply at least conversational German/French/Italian (matching the local language region) is a de facto hiring requirement even though no explicit legal minimum was found in official SEM/SBFI text.

### Gaps
- No explicit, sourced statement of a legal minimum language-proficiency level (e.g., CEFR A2/B1) tied to blue-collar work-permit issuance was found; this is inferred from sector norms, not confirmed by a primary source, and should be flagged as such in the final report.
- Did not locate a primary SEM page enumerating the exact current (2026) contingent numbers by canton or sector for third-country blue-collar permits (only secondary commentary was retrieved); a direct fetch of sem.admin.ch's contingent tables would strengthen this section.
