## UK income tax and National Insurance facts (2026/27 tax year), single filer, no dependents

These are for converting gross pay from rope-access/offshore postings (many of which are day-rate contractor roles) into net take-home pay. Figures are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland (Scotland has separate income tax bands, not covered here unless noted).

### Income tax (PAYE / employed and self-employed alike — income tax bands are the same regardless of employment status)
- **Personal Allowance: £12,570** — tax-free, per year. Tapered away (reduced £1 for every £2 of income) once total income exceeds £100,000, reaching £0 at £125,140.
- **Basic Rate: 20%** on income from £12,570 to £50,270.
- **Higher Rate: 40%** on income from £50,270 to £125,140.
- **Additional Rate: 45%** on income above £125,140.
- These thresholds are unchanged for 2026/27, continuing a freeze that (per the House of Commons Library and multiple tax advisory sources) is currently expected to last until 2031.
- Source: [The Private Office — UK Tax Allowances and Brackets for 2026/27](https://www.theprivateoffice.com/tax-planning/uk-tax-allowances-and-brackets); [TaxFly — Income Tax Rates & Bands 2026/27](https://www.taxfly.co.uk/guides/uk-income-tax-rates-and-bands-2026-27); [House of Commons Library — Direct taxes: Rates and allowances for 2026/27](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10618/)

### Employee National Insurance (Class 1 — for PAYE/permanent roles)
- **Rate: 8%** on earnings between the Primary Threshold and the Upper Earnings Limit; a lower rate (2%) applies above the Upper Earnings Limit (standard UK NI structure — the search sources confirm the 8% main rate for 2026/27 but did not give the exact reduced upper-band percentage; treat the "above UEL" rate as a gap, likely 2% per the long-standing structure, but verify against gov.uk before using it in calculations).
- **Primary Threshold: £242/week (£1,048/month, ≈£12,570/year)** — earnings below this are NI-free.
- **Upper Earnings Limit: £967/week (£4,189/month, ≈£50,270/year)**.
- **Lower Earnings Limit: £129/week (£559/month)** — below the Primary Threshold but above this, NI is "treated as paid" for state pension/benefit purposes without an actual deduction.
- Employer NI (context only, doesn't affect employee take-home directly but affects umbrella-company deductions — see below): 15% above a £5,000 secondary threshold.
- Source: [LITRG — National Insurance for employees](https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/employment/national-insurance-employees); [Employers Calculator — National Insurance Rates 2026/27](https://employerscalculator.co.uk/guides/national-insurance-rates-2026-27); [gov.uk — National Insurance rates and categories](https://www.gov.uk/national-insurance-rates-letters) (listed by search but not directly fetchable in this session — egress to gov.uk was blocked by the network proxy; treat as a source to verify manually)

### Self-employed / contractor status — how it changes the picture
Rope-access and offshore day-rate work is very often structured as self-employed, limited-company contracting, or "inside/outside IR35" umbrella arrangements rather than direct PAYE employment. This materially changes net-pay math:

- **Class 4 National Insurance (self-employed):** 6% on annual profits between £12,570 and £50,270; 2% on profits above £50,270. Source: [FreeAgent — Class 4 National Insurance rates for 2026/27](https://www.freeagent.com/rates/national-insurance-class-4/); [TakeHomeIncome — Class 2 vs Class 4 National Insurance 2026/27](https://takehomeincome.com/blog/class-2-vs-class-4-national-insurance-self-employed-2026-27)
- **Class 2 National Insurance (self-employed):** No longer compulsory for anyone. If profits are £7,105 or more (the 2026/27 Small Profits Threshold), the NI year is credited as if paid, at no cost. Below that threshold, paying is optional/voluntary at £3.65/week, to protect the State Pension record. Source: same as above.
- **Expense deductions:** A genuinely self-employed contractor (sole trader or via their own limited company, working *outside IR35*) can deduct allowable business expenses (travel to different sites, tools, PPE, accountancy fees, some subsistence) before tax — this can meaningfully reduce taxable profit compared to a PAYE employee, who generally cannot claim equivalent deductions.
- **IR35 / "inside IR35" status:** If HMRC or the engaging business determines the role is really "disguised employment" (the worker is supervised/directed/controlled like an employee, despite invoicing via a limited company or umbrella), the contractor is taxed via PAYE-equivalent rules on that income — income tax and NI are deducted at source similarly to an employee, but **without employee benefits (holiday pay, pension contributions, sick pay) and without the ability to claim travel/subsistence expense relief** (this expense restriction for supervised umbrella workers has applied since April 2016).
- **Umbrella companies:** A common arrangement for inside-IR35 contractors, especially in offshore/oil & gas and wind. The umbrella company employs the worker on paper and processes PAYE income tax and employee NI, but first deducts Employer NI (15% above the £5,000 threshold) and the Apprenticeship Levy (0.5%) from the assignment/day rate *before* calculating the worker's gross pay — so the day rate quoted by a recruiter is not the same as the gross salary the tax/NI calculation should be applied to. Example found in research: a £500/day contract (220 working days = £110,000 contract value) nets roughly £59,204/year (≈£4,934/month) take-home after all umbrella deductions, tax and NI — illustrating that headline day rates can overstate real take-home pay by a wide margin. Source: [uktaxdrag.co.uk — An inside-IR35 contractor's full money picture, 2026/27](https://uktaxdrag.co.uk/inside-ir35-contractor-money-guide-2026-27.html)
- **From 6 April 2026:** New rules (Finance Act 2026) let HMRC recover unpaid PAYE/NI from the end client or recruitment agency if an umbrella company fails to remit it, shifting liability up the supply chain — relevant context for recruiters/agencies used in this market but does not change an individual technician's own net-pay calculation.
- Source: [Holloway Davies — IR35 Explained for Umbrella Company Contractors](https://www.hollowaydavies.co.uk/blog/payroll-and-paye/ir35-explained-umbrella-company-contractors); [uktaxcal.com — Inside IR35 Calculator 2026/27](https://uktaxcal.com/profiles/inside-ir35.html)
